The rule

One concave solid.
Eight smaller chairs.

Remove one corner from a 2 × 2 × 2 cube and regard the boundary of the remaining volume as one concave polyhedron. Inflate that chair by two. The enlarged solid admits an exact cover by eight congruent chairs, including one translated central child. Repeating the rule builds a nested three-dimensional hierarchy.

Hierarchy versus shape

The inflation is nonperiodic.
The bare tile is not.

The unmarked heptacube can also assemble periodically, so its geometry alone does not force this hierarchy. The search above replaces it by a finite set of two-level collared chairs. Each exposed unit face carries a socket; a move is legal only when all touching sockets are complementary and the new chair does not overlap the patch.

What is proved here—and what is not

The executable catalogue reconstructs a 64-chair, depth-two supertile without being handed its boundary. It demonstrates the GCTS encoding, but it is not yet a proof that this compact catalogue forces the infinite hierarchy. Goodman–Strauss guarantees that a finite forcing decoration can be constructed under the theorem’s hypotheses; establishing that our decoration is one requires a unique-composition audit at every scale. The well-known explicit chair rules are for the planar L-chair; these generated collars belong to its 3D heptacube analogue.
