01

The rule

One concave solid.
Eight smaller chairs.

Remove one corner from a 2 × 2 × 2 cube and regard the boundary of the remaining volume as one concave polyhedron. Inflate that chair by two. The enlarged solid admits an exact cover by eight congruent chairs, including one translated central child. Repeating the rule builds a nested three-dimensional hierarchy.

02

Hierarchy versus shape

The inflation is nonperiodic.
The bare tile is not.

The unmarked heptacube can also assemble periodically, so its geometry alone does not force this hierarchy. A GCTS version must carry finite local state—face markings, child addresses, or collared neighborhoods—that makes every legal patch compose uniquely into larger chairs.
