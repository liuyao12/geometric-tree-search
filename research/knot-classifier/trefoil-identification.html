A trefoil recognized in three moves

The search identifies a six-crossing diagram with the standard three-crossing trefoil. The diagrams are specified independently as braid closures; the solver receives their signed Gauss words and bounds, with no reference move sequence.

Why these endpoints are known to agree

The source is the closure of the three-strand braid \(\sigma_1(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^2\sigma_1^{-1}\). Conjugation preserves the closure. The braid relation gives \[(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^2=\sigma_1(\sigma_2\sigma_1\sigma_2)=\sigma_1^2\sigma_2\sigma_1.\] Cyclic conjugation gives \(\sigma_1^3\sigma_2\), whose Markov destabilization is the two-strand braid \(\sigma_1^3\), the target trefoil. See Schleimer's braid and Markov definitions and Ranicki's trefoil braid example. Both endpoints also have Fox coloring dimension \(2\) over \(\mathbb F_3\), whereas the unknot has dimension \(1\).

Verification and algorithm scope

The JavaScript move enumerator replays every tile. A separate Python implementation independently checks all three lane certificates, including planar face conditions and endpoints. Changing a type-III tile into a type-I deletion is rejected. The shared point engine is unchanged: full bipartite bookkeeping, global dead-end checks, global forced moves, generation scheduling, and exact rollback use the existing conformance-tested implementation.

Failure guards are proved only for this target, time horizon, and crossing cap. Their synthesis remains the specialized exhaustive seam-reachability control described in the research plan. This test advances the positive-equivalence benchmark; local geometric marking synthesis and collision-free spatial lifts remain open subgoals.
