Classify lattice knots.

The research target is a classifier whose states are exact piecewise-linear embedded circles in the cubic lattice \(\mathbb Z^3\). The search assembles lattice realizations of Reidemeister tiles in space-time. Projection and the knot movie present the resulting certificate; they do not replace the lattice state or determine search bounds.

The earlier signed-diagram seam solver is a correctness control. Its successful trefoil movie does not establish a lattice move sequence or a classifier. The renderer's crossing limit is a presentation limit and has no role in the classifier.

Implemented: lattice moves and a nontrivial identification

The classifier now searches exact lattice embeddings using reversible empty-square disk sweeps. The tile adapter decorates a sweep as Reidemeister I, II or III only when an independent projected endpoint check admits that single diagram move; unchanged projected diagrams are routing tiles. Types I and II allow both expansion and reduction. Geometry stays in the inventory and verifier. The generic brute-force engine receives only candidate identifiers, point capacities and opaque seam markings.

The first test identifies two different embeddings of the trefoil. The source is independently certified from the KnotInfo reference by a 15-tile lattice provenance movie. In a fixed four-square move-support patch, the neutral search discovers a five-tile sequence: II reduction, II expansion, III, II expansion, I reduction. The universe has 26 layer-state entries and 53 transition placements. Both lanes visit 7 search nodes, make 5 branch decisions and backtrack twice. The online lane starts empty, learns 2 proved failure guards from fresh unmarked probes, and records 4 candidate eliminations due to those guards. Independent Python replay checks the exact spatial exterior, square disk, collars, rational projection and every declared diagram move.

This is a small finite classifier test, not recognition coverage through 12 crossings or evidence of speedup. The search is not given the construction path. Endpoint diagram checks and spatial isotopy are certified; a claim that a continuous projection has exactly one event per tile needs additional event analysis.

Reference types through 12 crossings

A new corpus contains 2,977 prime reference knot types and the unknot, lifted from KnotInfo grid notation. Horizontal segments lie at height zero, vertical segments at height one, and endpoint connectors join the two levels. All coordinates are integers; every edge follows a coordinate axis. Every polygon is closed and self-avoiding, with 4–200 unit edges. Mirrors are generated explicitly and never identified by a reflection in the state key.

Exact lattice corpus (JSON) · Validation and provenance. Only names, crossing labels and grid coordinates were extracted from the published workbook. Labels are evaluator metadata: searchInput removes them and supplies only vertices.

This is the first milestone, not the full meaning of “all knots through 12 crossings”: the current corpus covers prime types and the unknot. Composite types must be added from an independently checked source. Mirror and orientation conventions must also be scored explicitly; the initial state contract is unoriented and preserves spatial handedness. The source grid may project with more crossings than the reference's minimal crossing number.

What the search is allowed to know

The generic algorithm receives point functions, matching values, candidate identifiers and declared budgets. It does not receive knot names, crossing-number labels, coloring or polynomial invariants, braid relations, known untangling paths, or move-type preferences. The inventory author and independent verifier know the definition of a legal tile. Reference labels are attached only after a verified match. The available tile inventory includes temporary increases in lattice length and projection complexity.

RL is deferred until this lattice model is working. If restored, it should learn proposals from point/collar data and verified search outcomes, retain complete base-tile fallback, and be evaluated separately from pure brute force plus GCTS.

Bounds and classification claims

“Through 12 crossings” means the target knot types have minimal crossing number at most \(12\). It does not impose a 12-crossing limit on intermediate diagrams. A run has separate bounds on spatial box size, lattice edge count, refinement and move horizon. Those bounds must be increased fairly across attempts. Search seams quotient cyclic starting vertex and traversal reversal while preserving the exact spatial coordinate frame. Translation-normalized keys remain available for corpus geometry checks, but do not silently move the knot within a bounded search. Reflection and rescaling are not quotiented.

A verified lattice movie to a reference is a positive identification. Exhausting one finite box, horizon or edge bound leaves classification unknown. Learned hypotheses cannot turn this into an inequivalence claim. Search over increasing bounds is a positive recognition approach; it does not by itself guarantee termination for inputs outside the reference bank or distinguish every negative pair in finite time. Classification coverage on the finite benchmark is an empirical acceptance gate, not a theorem about arbitrary lattice inputs.

Tile geometry and presentation

Keep the spatial knot in \(\mathbb Z^3\) and time as an additional coordinate. Four-dimensional cubical blocks can carry the exact spatial history; three-dimensional hexagonal prisms can display its projection along \((1,1,1)\). A Reidemeister tile may require a refined block rather than one unit cell. Every claimed move needs an actual isotopy, not merely a surface joining its endpoints. The viewer consumes saved lattice certificates and projects them afterward; aesthetic layout cannot supply or alter search states.

Local cubic-lattice plaquette moves have a useful established isotopy theory; see Janse van Rensburg and Rechnitzer. The current inventory filters these geometric sweeps by the Reidemeister endpoint test, rather than treating every sweep as a Reidemeister move. This restricted inventory is not claimed complete for arbitrary lattice isotopy.

For horizon \(H\), each transition covers one time-slot point with exact value \(1\) and assigns its complete before/after lattice embedding at two marking-only seams. The fixed boundary assigns source and target seams. All required slots are roots of generation zero. The generic engine maintains complete forward/reverse incidence, checks global dead ends before forced moves, branches only after propagation, and reconstructs the entire graph after rollback or a marking version change.

Observed failures trigger fresh unmarked completion probes with only the candidate fixed. Complete exhaustion proves redundancy for this exact finite target, inventory, coordinate frame and horizon. The boundary then assigns \(0\) at a new marking point and the candidate assigns \(1\). Budget exhaustion creates no marking. These are ordinary assigned point values, not a runtime failure-clause scanner. Saved benchmark values are evidence and never loaded as a prior.

The implementation is a finite whole-state seam adaptation of the master contract. It does not implement local spatial point functions, neighboring-pair corona marking synthesis, transferable constraints, RL, or a complete knot decision procedure. Catalog truncation and resource stops yield unknown. The square-face grammar excludes degenerate fixed-direction projections and multi-move projected endpoint changes; its exhaustive enumeration claim applies only to that declared grammar. The positive classifier tries supplied unlabeled reference geometry and attaches its evaluator name only after successful spatial replay.
