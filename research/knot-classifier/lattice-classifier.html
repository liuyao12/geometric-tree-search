Classify lattice knots.

The research target is a classifier whose states are exact piecewise-linear embedded circles in the cubic lattice \(\mathbb Z^3\). The current classifier searches for paths of checked lattice moves between exact embeddings. Learned point markings prune that graph search. Projection and the knot movie present the resulting certificate; they do not replace the lattice state or determine search bounds.

The earlier signed-diagram seam solver is a correctness control. Its successful trefoil movie does not establish a lattice move sequence or a classifier. The renderer's crossing limit is a presentation limit and has no role in the classifier.

The selected formulation: lattice move paths with GCTS markings

The classifier searches the graph of exact lattice embeddings. A node is a whole knot; an edge is a checked reversible lattice move. A bidirectional search grows from both endpoints and joins two parent paths. This directly matches the equivalence question and does not require filling a space-time volume. A verified path can still be rendered as a knot movie.

The engine sees opaque keys, complete candidate domains and point values. At each state there is one choice point, with unit \(t\) for every move. Seam \(m\)-values encode opaque endpoint agreement. Online local extension failures produce proved failed-budget \(m\)-values; boundary disagreement removes candidates through the point/candidate graph. Unknown probes add no exclusions. These are real candidate markings in a declared graph-path adaptation. Space-time exact tiling and the reference neighboring-tile/corona learner remain separate formulations.

No knot name, invariant, crossing label, ancestor, scramble history or preferred move type enters search. The author adapter and independent verifier define legal lattice moves. Every incident face throughout the knot remains available under the existing fixed-projection grammar and finite edge bounds. Learned profiles are validated once and kept immutable, preserving the earlier transfer-cost improvement.

Testing a different tree scheduler

Balanced scheduling expands the shallower breadth-first wave. Adaptive scheduling expands the smaller pending frontier, with depth breaking ties. Each wave retains complete layer enumeration and shortest-prefix bookkeeping. As the two radii grow, any allowed path within their sum crosses both retained balls, so changing which side expands preserves bounded path existence. This is a specialized graph scheduler; the reference tiling generation rule is not claimed.

A new corpus contains 2,977 prime reference knot types and the unknot, lifted from KnotInfo grid notation. What the search is allowed to know

The generic algorithm receives point functions, matching values, candidate identifiers and declared budgets. It does not receive knot names, crossing-number labels, coloring or polynomial invariants, braid relations, known untangling paths, or move-type preferences. The inventory author and independent verifier know the definition of a legal tile. Reference labels are attached only after a verified match. The available tile inventory includes temporary increases in lattice length and projection complexity.

RL is deferred until this lattice model is working. If restored, it should learn proposals from point/collar data and verified search outcomes, retain complete base-tile fallback, and be evaluated separately from pure brute force plus GCTS.

Bounds and classification claims

“Through 12 crossings” means the target knot types have minimal crossing number at most \(12\). It does not impose a 12-crossing limit on intermediate diagrams. A run has separate bounds on spatial box size, lattice edge count, refinement and move horizon. Those bounds must be increased fairly across attempts. Search seams quotient cyclic starting vertex and traversal reversal while preserving the exact spatial coordinate frame. Translation-normalized keys remain available for corpus geometry checks, but do not silently move the knot within a bounded search. Reflection and rescaling are not quotiented.

A verified lattice movie to a reference is a positive identification. Exhausting one finite box, horizon or edge bound leaves classification unknown. Learned hypotheses cannot turn this into an inequivalence claim. Search over increasing bounds is a positive recognition approach; it does not by itself guarantee termination for inputs outside the reference bank or distinguish every negative pair in finite time. Classification coverage on the finite benchmark is an empirical acceptance gate, not a theorem about arbitrary lattice inputs.

Tile geometry and presentation

Keep the spatial knot in \(\mathbb Z^3\) and time as an additional coordinate. Four-dimensional cubical blocks can carry the exact spatial history; three-dimensional hexagonal prisms can display its projection along \((1,1,1)\). A Reidemeister tile may require a refined block rather than one unit cell. Every claimed move needs an actual isotopy, not merely a surface joining its endpoints. The viewer consumes saved lattice certificates and projects them afterward; aesthetic layout cannot supply or alter search states.

Local cubic-lattice plaquette moves have a useful established isotopy theory; see Janse van Rensburg and Rechnitzer. The current inventory filters these geometric sweeps by the Reidemeister endpoint test, rather than treating every sweep as a Reidemeister move. This restricted inventory is not claimed complete for arbitrary lattice isotopy.
