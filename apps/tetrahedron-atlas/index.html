THE TELLTALE DIFFERENCE

Similar neighbours.
Different long-range order.

The quasicrystal has twelvefold diffraction. Its approximant breaks that symmetry to fourfold. Local resemblance does not imply global repetition.

A DENSITY DETAIL

The cell is not the record.

The interactive approximant uses the paper’s published 82-particle cell, at 84.7866%. The reported 85.0267% result came from separately compressing an eight-cell, 656-particle supercell. Simply repeating these 82 coordinates does not reproduce that higher density.

The original ideal starting cell and time history are not included in the available coordinate data. The expanded-reference animation preserves the endpoint orientations and closes extra spacing; it does not reconstruct the authors’ relaxation. Its underlying vertex arrangement is \((3,4,3^2,4)\). ANOTHER APERIODIC ROUTE

Icosahedral order.

Fang and colleagues proposed a different regular-tetrahedron quasicrystal, built by decorating a three-dimensional Ammann tiling. Its reported density is 59.783%. It is a useful example of choosing symmetry and aperiodicity over maximum density.

WHY EXPLORE THIS?

Density is one objective.

For hard tetrahedra, the 2011 phase-diagram study found the approximant more stable than the denser dimer crystal below about 84% packing fraction. Entropy makes structure selection richer than a race to fill every gap.
