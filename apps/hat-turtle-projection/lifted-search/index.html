Why several lifts can give the same hat move

In integer triangular coordinates, the two projections are

\[H(p,q,r,s)=(p+r-s,\ q+r+2s),\qquad T(p,q,r,s)=(2p+q+r,\ -p+q+s).\]

For a fixed hat translation, every lifted translation has the form

\[a+m(-1,-1,1,0)+n(1,-2,0,1),\qquad m,n\in\mathbb Z.\]

The extra terms vanish under the hat projection, while moving the turtle by \((-2m,-2n)\). Fixing both projected translations leaves at most one lift.

Here the first tile is fixed. Whenever a new tile shares a projected vertex, its four-dimensional coordinate must agree with the one already there. This determines the lift. The search considers every orientation and every boundary-point alignment that can fill the frontier deficit.

What surface the tiles make

The height coordinates can be chosen as \((r,s)\). Given the hat position \((x,y)\), the other coordinates are recovered by

\[p=x-r+s,\qquad q=y-r-2s.\]

Thus a complete coherent lift is a graph of two height functions over the hat tiling. Vertices are in \(\mathbb Z^4\); the filled triangles lie in \(\mathbb R^4\). An entire tiled plane with a valid triangulation would lift to a surface homeomorphic to the plane, possibly with creases. Finite search patches may have holes or boundary pinches.

What is being searched and verified

This is a connected, coherently lifted point-value search. Both projections have exact capacity tests: vertex angles use integer twelfths, and a common subdivision into small triangles detects positive-area overlap. The four-dimensional coordinates are problem-defining equality markings on boundary vertices.

The scheduler checks all active points for dead ends, then propagates forced moves, then branches at the earliest generation. Every branch restores point totals, markings, generations and candidate domains. No substitution rule, learned marking or catalog tiling guides this search.
