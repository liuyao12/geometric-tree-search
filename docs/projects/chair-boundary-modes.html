The result and its scope

Allow integer translations and all twenty-four proper cubic rotations of the exact seven-cube chair, excluding reflections. Each copy carries the same prescribed total field in its own coordinates, with fixed amplitude. Require scalar values to agree on every shared unit boundary panel. This has exactly the Chair44 arrow-rule tilings. The cited Chair44 theorem supplies infinite existence and excludes every nonzero translational period of the decorated tiling. [1]

Here the rule encodes the chair's own hierarchy of supertiles. A single prototype field replaces an arbitrary menu of independently colored tiles. The ordinary boundary condition is homogeneous Neumann, \(\partial_\nu u=0\); restricting the modal coefficients to this prescribed field is an additional matching constraint. Arbitrary superpositions, or the Neumann condition alone, do not force aperiodicity.

A scalar profile remembers an arrow and its color

The chair is the interior of the union of the closed unit cubes

\[ \overline\Omega=\bigcup_{c\in C}\bigl(c+[0,1]^3\bigr),\qquad C=\{0,1\}^3\setminus\{(1,1,1)\}. \]

It has volume \(7\) and \(24\) exposed unit panels. The given decoration assigns each panel an outward unit normal \(n\), a nonzero diagonal tangent arrow \(a\), and a color. Set

\[ \sigma=\begin{cases}1,&\text{red},\\-1,&\text{green},\\0,&\text{blue},\end{cases}\qquad b=\sigma n,\qquad d=b\times a,\qquad |a|^2=2. \]

Across a common panel, the arrow rule requires equal tangent arrows and either red/green or blue/blue colors. Opposite outward normals turn this complementary-color requirement into equality of \(b\). This normal vector can be carried by the tangent vector \(d\), because

\[ b=\frac{a\times d}{2}. \]

For tangential displacement \(y\) from the panel center, use

\[ B(y)=\begin{cases}\exp\!\left(1-\dfrac1{1-16|y|^2}\right),&|y|<1/4,\\0,&|y|\ge1/4,\end{cases}\qquad F_{a,b}(y)=B(y)\bigl(a\cdot y+16|y|^2(d\cdot y)\bigr). \]

The linear and cubic terms independently carry two tangent vectors. This is one scalar profile; the displayed red/blue intensity of the function is not the source arrow's color.

The full field and its boundary condition

For each panel center \(p_j\), let \(r_j(x)=(x-p_j)\cdot n_j\) and \(y_j(x)=x-p_j-r_j(x)n_j\). Define

\[ K(z)=\begin{cases}\exp\!\left(1-\dfrac1{1-z^2}\right),&|z|<1,\\0,&|z|\ge1,\end{cases}\qquad u(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{24}F_{a_j,b_j}\bigl(y_j(x)\bigr)K\bigl(4r_j(x)\bigr). \]

Each open support fits inside a ball with squared radius \(1/8\). Distinct panel centers have squared separation at least \(1/2\), so those open balls are disjoint, even in the equality case. The squared distance from each center to any other panel is at least \(1/4\), so its support avoids every other boundary panel. On its own panel the trace is exactly \(F_{a_j,b_j}\), and its normal derivative is zero since \(K'(0)=0\). All fields vanish near unit-panel edges and corners.

Why boundary equality is exactly the given matching rule

  1. On the common disk, divide equal traces by the positive common factor \(B\). Equality becomes equality of a polynomial with linear and cubic homogeneous parts.
  2. Varying the radius along every tangent direction identifies those parts separately, giving \(a=a'\) and \(d=d'\). Both coefficient vectors are tangent, so no invisible normal component remains.
  3. The recovery identity then gives \(b=b'\), exactly the original arrow and complementary-color rule. The converse follows directly from the formula.
  4. For a proper rotation \(R\), \((Rb)\times(Ra)=R(b\times a)\). The radial bumps and dot products also commute with rotation. Thus each oriented field is the scalar pullback of the same prototype; no independent vector channel travels between tiles.
  5. The local correspondence holds on every capacity-legal patch, hence on every full tiling. The known Chair44 hierarchy theorem applies to the decoded arrows and excludes a decorated translation period. The infinite conclusion does not follow merely from the finite audit.

The exact audit checks all \(864\) facing-panel direction/color cases and all \(3{,}000\) relative rotation/translation cases in the full face-neighbor range. It recovers exactly \(44\) legal face neighbors. The field matches on the substitution patches with \(1,8,64,512\) chairs. Construction and contact receipt.

Equal scalar values give equal tangential derivatives, and the two zero normal derivatives cancel flux. The prescribed collar profiles also have identical even normal extensions across a matched seam. These are static multiple-frequency fields, not solutions of one global Helmholtz equation.

Which modes occur?

\[ -\Delta\phi_k=\mu_k\phi_k,\qquad\partial_\nu\phi_k=0,\qquad u=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}c_k\phi_k,\qquad c_k=\int_\Omega u\phi_k\,dx. \]

The ordinary Neumann eigenbasis is orthonormal in \(L^2(\Omega)\). This smooth field has convergence in the Neumann form norm, equivalent to \(H^1\), and its boundary traces converge in \(H^{1/2}\). Every collar is odd under tangential inversion, so its integral is zero. Consequently the coefficient of the constant mode is exactly \(c_0=0\).

There is also a regular exact subfamily:

\[ \phi_m(x)=\frac{2^{r(m)/2}}{\sqrt7}\prod_{i=1}^{3}\cos(\pi m_ix_i),\qquad \mu_m=\pi^2\sum_{i=1}^{3}m_i^2,\qquad m\in\mathbb N_0^3, \]

where \(r(m)\) counts nonzero coordinates. Every boundary plane has an integer coordinate, so the normal sine derivative vanishes. Orthogonality and the norm follow by subtracting the missing unit cube from the full cube. This is a subfamily of the chair spectrum, not its full basis or aperiodicity test. The FEM computation uses low members as independent numerical controls.

The numerical eigenfunctions have arbitrary signs and noncanonical bases inside multiple eigenspaces. Individual coefficients depend on those choices; the squared norm captured by a whole eigenspace does not.

Finite-mode limits and a different tolerance rule

The target is nonzero and vanishes on an open set in the connected interior of the chair. A finite sum of Laplacian eigenfunctions is real analytic in that interior, so it cannot equal this field. The exact carrier necessarily has infinitely many modes. This does not rule out another exact finite-mode construction, which remains unresolved here.

A separate finite-mode existence result uses a fixed \(L^2\) tolerance on each shared unit panel, without extra constraints at isolated edges or vertices. The oriented trace alphabet is finite, and the profile identity gives a positive minimum separation \(d\) between distinct symbols. Trace convergence supplies one finite continuum cutoff \(N\) with error below \(d/4\) on every panel of every oriented state. Intended neighbors then have discrepancy below \(d/2\); forbidden neighbors have discrepancy above \(d/2\). Accepting exactly those contacts within the tolerance preserves the original discrete rule.

All finite sums still satisfy Neumann conditions. This changed rule gives approximate seams, rather than continuous gluing. No cutoff has been computed or certified, and the displayed FEM truncations are not asserted to meet it.
