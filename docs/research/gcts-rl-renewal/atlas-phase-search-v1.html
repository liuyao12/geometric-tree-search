Minimize excess coverage over the candidate family

A phase is one whole-plane tiling after a fixed lattice translation and an optional half-turn. Extract the whole tiles with positive-area intersection with the core. Each phase then determines one patch and one outer boundary.

For Turtle the score is the tile count. For Penrose the two rhomb areas give an exact score in \(\mathbb{Z}[\phi]\) under a common area normalization. A branch can stop when the already witnessed tiles reach the incumbent score. A complete candidate is checked for coverage, and each partial candidate is checked by replaying hierarchy addresses and exact intersections.

Learn which boundary segment to probe first

A point strictly inside a tile cannot lie on the boundary of a union of whole tiles from that phase. Probe the midpoint of an exposed target segment. If it lies in a tile’s interior, the tile crosses the target boundary. The certificate records a hierarchy address and the exact strict-interior test.

The learner ranks segment features by verified rejections per hierarchy visit in training. Each held-out phase gets up to two probes. A phase without a crossing witness goes to the same full extraction as the baseline.
