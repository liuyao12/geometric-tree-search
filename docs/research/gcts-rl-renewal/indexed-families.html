The index changes construction, not the mathematical problem.

Each needed family position is matched once against the entire ground rule inventory. Subsequent queries intersect exact formula-parameter assignments; available hypotheses and actually occupied earlier lines are indexed by formula. Aggregates remain the sum and compatible union of all original constituents. Repeated expansions and deterministic ordering hashes can reuse per-solve caches.

Indexes are constructed lazily after global propagation selects a genuine branch. Every solve starts with a fresh model and empty caches. A model/library snapshot check rejects changed inputs. These are immutable solve-local indexes; dynamic marking changes and incremental cache invalidation are outside this experiment.

The global scheduler, complete base domains, retained family phases, unit capacities and fallback are unchanged. The independently enumerated pool and every fixed-controller tree are identical. The index never supplies a degree-zero or degree-one assertion and adds no failure marking.

Assessment: cheaper joins in some cases, no demonstrated RL advantage.
The indexed fixed join lowers the median relative to enumeration on the arithmetic, incidence, decoy and compound-decoy controls. It raises the median on the short fork, compound fork, original longer proof and new five-tail case. Arithmetic and incidence use no learned family; their saving is reduced overhead from unsuccessful joins. Both remain slower than base search.
On the new eight-inference example, base needs \(759\) attempts and fixed and trained family controllers \(129\). The measured medians are approximately \(108\) ms for base, \(104\) ms for enumeration, \(116\) ms for indexed fixed and \(95\) ms for indexed RL. The fixed and learned controllers choose exactly the same families and placements, with equal non-time metrics on every evaluation. Their overlapping, bimodal timings do not support attributing the lower RL median to learning. Saturation's single-observation time is lower than every positive point-lane median, including this example.
