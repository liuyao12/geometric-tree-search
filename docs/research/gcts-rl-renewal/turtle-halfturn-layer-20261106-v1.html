Recovering higher Golden Hex Turtle layers

A search starting from the previously recovered Turtle motifs built five new patches. The largest contains 5,654 Turtles. A five-child contact pattern supplied the larger frames, and a rule fitted to two recovered strip words predicted the next two words. Both predictions fit their frames exactly.

The half-turn template uses one central child and two opposed pairs. At each new scale, the search places children at exposed-edge contacts and rejects overlaps using reusable forbidden displacements. Three representative seams of the selected frame grow from (5) matched segments at the original scale to (17), (41), and (103). Opposite seams repeat by symmetry.

At the two predicted scales, the exposed boundary of the five-child frame gives two interior cavity rings. Each ring matches an earlier triangle after edge reversal. The search installs those triangles as caps, reuses the corner attachments from the triangle assembly, and then places a strip and its half-turn image.

The best motif depends on where it goes

Starting with the 18-tile motif (P_1), the search enumerated 24 closed five-child cores and fitted mirrored strips. Restricting the core search to the largest seam score returned the same 30 oriented completed motifs as the exhaustive core search. These gave 10 shapes up to isometry.

For each candidate, the search assembled a triangle with three copies of the candidate, three earlier triangles (T_2), and a center cap (T_1). It measured each boundary by its count (p) of exposed primitive lattice segments and used (p^2/n), with (n) the tile count, to compare shapes. The 124-tile motif ranked fifth on its own and first in the triangle. The 122-tile motif ranked first on its own and second in the triangle.

Predict the next strip before fitting it

The earlier strip stencil places one oriented Turtle per letter along a fixed lattice stride. The 18-tile and 124-tile motifs give strip words (s_2=mathtt{0010}) and (s_4=mathtt{00100010010}). We assumed a family defined by (s_{j+1}=s_js_{j-1}) with two rounds per layer. In that family (s_4=s_2s_1s_2), which determines (s_1=mathtt{001}). From (s_2=s_1s_0) we obtain (s_0=mathtt{0}).

That fit predicts a 29-letter word (s_6) and a 76-letter word (s_8). For each trial the search fixed the predicted word, then checked translations and orientations along the frame boundary. The successful placements have disjoint tile interiors and close a single outer ring.

The word family is a modeling choice. Two recovered words can fit multiple rules. The subsequent geometry supplies two independent finite-scale checks of this particular prediction.
