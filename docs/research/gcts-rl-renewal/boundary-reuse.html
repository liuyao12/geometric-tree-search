What the comparison can establish.

A point representation assigns opposite values at a private distant point to the two sets of writers. A candidate that writes both words receives a separate fixed exclusion point. The direct control keeps the original tile markings and stores the same writer conjunctions as clauses. Selecting a writer updates clause-to-candidate dependencies in the shared graph. It has no additional lookahead or arc consistency.

There is a simple representation equivalence. Let \(K\) be the selected placements, \(L_i\) and \(R_i\) the left-only and right-only writers, and \(J_i\) the shared writers for a clause. Its extra point values agree exactly when

\[ K\cap J_i=\varnothing \quad\text{and}\quad \bigl(K\cap L_i=\varnothing\ \text{or}\ K\cap R_i=\varnothing\bigr). \]

A shared writer disagrees with its fixed exclusion point. Selecting both a left writer and a right writer gives unequal values at their common point; either side alone agrees. The direct predicate tests exactly these cases. Private points are distinct across clauses, so this applies to their conjunction. With the same original point rules and deterministic base scheduler, equal candidate legality gives equal search trees. The measurements test whether the implementations realize this equivalence; they do not claim a new logical principle.

The direct comparison uses base ordering. Fixed and learned ordering are measured separately on the original and point models. Their feature vectors include marking counts, so they are not a pure representation comparison. All candidates, complete frontier incidence, global dead and forced propagation, earliest-generation branching, fallback and exact rollback remain in force.
