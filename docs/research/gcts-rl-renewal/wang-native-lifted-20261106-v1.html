From a clause with variables to a checked fragment

  1. Propose a typed substitution
  2. Encode open variables as fresh constants
  3. Check the local tile fragment
  4. Activate the derived clause

The given-clause prover proposes complementary literals and a substitution. Each parent is encoded on the local top boundary as a clause schema. The move instantiates both parents and then resolves them using the established tape instructions. For factoring, one instantiation makes the two selected literals identical. Their duplicate occurrence represents one member of the resulting clause.

Open variables in the proposed result receive fresh, distinct parameter codes. Because those codes do not occur in the parent schemas, replacing them consistently with terms of the declared sorts preserves the inference. This allows the checked result to enter search as a clause with variables. The replay decodes each scope map and checks its types, parent identities, and conclusion. The tape checks the encoded instances and resolution.

A newly proposed clause may wait unverified in the queue. Before it becomes an active parent, the search checks its derivation against already admitted parents or normalized source clauses. A contradiction is checked before it can end the search. The local fragment ends at a proof-line checkpoint. The full proof reaches the accepting cap.
