How this is Wang-style, and what the squares mean
An ordinary Wang tile has a square body and colors on four edges. Here a proof cell is represented by one capacity point, and a selected tile assigns formula values at its own output port and at the ports of its earlier premises. These markings can reach beyond adjacent cells. A searched cluster combines several base tiles; its outer ownership markings make the cluster occupy its cells as one placement. Every displayed value is reconstructed from the audited search result, with a link to the exact proof certificate.
The square outlines are a visualization of discrete occupancy, not an added continuous geometric constraint. These runs fill a finite strip of proof cells embedded in a two-dimensional point domain. The tile types are positional and problem-specific; we have not yet built a uniform finite inventory of four-edge Wang squares. A base search tile can expand into several primitive logical inferences. The parameterized donor proofs close their final equation by checked universal generalization after the point tiling.
