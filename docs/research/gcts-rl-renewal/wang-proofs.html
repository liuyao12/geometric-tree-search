How this is Wang-style, and what the squares mean
An ordinary Wang tile has a square body and colors on four edges. Here a proof cell is represented by one capacity point, and a selected tile assigns formula values at its own output port and at the ports of its earlier premises. These markings can reach beyond adjacent cells. A searched cluster combines several base tiles; its outer ownership markings make the cluster occupy its cells as one placement. Every displayed value is reconstructed from the audited search result, with a link to the exact proof certificate.
The square outlines are a visualization of discrete occupancy, not an added continuous geometric constraint. These runs fill a finite strip of proof cells embedded in a two-dimensional point domain. The tile types are positional and problem-specific; we have not yet built a uniform finite inventory of four-edge Wang squares. A base search tile can expand into several primitive logical inferences. The parameterized donor proofs close their final equation by checked universal generalization after the point tiling.

A proof is different from a search cutoff.
The short-envelope control uses a true arithmetic target with too few cells for this grammar. Exhausting that finite envelope does not show the theorem is false or unprovable. Other representation lanes can hit their wall budget even when the displayed positive lane finds a checked proof.
The English mechanically reads each formula, followed by a separate explanation of its inference rule. Brackets preserve nested implication and quantifier scope. This helps read the formal certificate; the English itself is not independently checked as a natural-language proof. The full primitive view expands every used lemma, remaps all premise references, and independently checks every resulting line. Root references are global in that expanded view; lemma definitions use local references and explicit local assumptions. Neither a lemma name nor a successful final line substitutes for checking the preceding derivation.
These are Wang-style proof tiles with exact occupancy and extended point markings. The present inventory is positional and finite for each problem, with problem-specific formula IDs. A uniform finite tile system and an unrestricted mathematical proof-search language are research goals. The underlying checker accepts a broader first-order language; the current search grammar is narrower. Useful RL acceleration and a distinctive GCTS advantage over classical search are still unproved.
