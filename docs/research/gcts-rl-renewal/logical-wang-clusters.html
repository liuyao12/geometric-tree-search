Check the unmarked tiling and decode its proof. Validity is independent of how the solution was found.
There is direct precedent: Golomb, Tiling with sets of polyominoes (1970) connects polyomino plane tiling to the Wang domino problem. Ollinger (2009) gives reductions using finitely many polyominoes, including a translation-only construction. Yang and Zhang (2024) explicitly encode Wang colors geometrically in a translation-only polyomino reduction. These establish a route for compiling computational matching into shape, not a practical proof-search advantage for our method.
Shape encodings need complementary boundary profiles and constraints forcing the intended macro alignment. A forward picture of interlocking pieces is insufficient: unintended offsets, reversed layouts and alternate decompositions must also be excluded or decoded. A plane-tiling undecidability theorem alone does not establish a fixed finite proof-certificate system with our chosen input and accepting boundaries. The finite-region compilation and its reverse decoding require their own proof. The color-to-shape conversion itself is established; checking cost and useful direct logical interfaces are the priorities below.
Using markings only to order moves is compatible with complete primitive fallback and fair search. A redundant marking proved from the geometric system may prune safely. An unproved learned marking may restrict exploration, but failure under that marking remains unknown for the unmarked problem. It cannot license a claim that no proof exists. A successful tiling that passes the unmarked checker remains valid even if heuristic pruning was unsound.
Separating learned search from trusted checking is established practice in automated theorem proving; HOList / DeepHOL (2019) provides an RL environment built on HOL Light. This literature supports the general separation. It does not establish the particular combination of polyomino clusters and GCTS markings as a proven improvement.
Research judgment: keep the common first-order language and one base inventory as the architectural goal. Study an uncolored polyomino realization as an alternative back end. First demonstrate forward and reverse decoding on a small finite proof region, with a verifier that ignores search markings, then run matched marked/unmarked and classical-search controls. No polyomino proof compiler has been implemented in this notebook.


Checking markings is a sparse agreement test.
The useful question is the cost of checking. Moving colors into notches is an established encoding and is not, by itself, a new research contribution. For \(M\) concrete marking assignments, a checker indexes each exact point/channel key, stores its first assigned value and checks later assignments for equality. A dictionary gives expected \(O(M)\) indexing work, plus the cost of exact value comparisons; sorting gives a deterministic \(O(M\log M)\) alternative. Occupancy is accumulated separately and the entire target is checked. A hash is an index, not an equality certificate.
Long-range support does not require testing every pair of tiles. Only overlapping assigned keys need comparison. The cost grows with the number and representation of assigned values, not directly with their geometric distance. Sparse receptors are preferable to copying the entire prior proof state onto every subsequent line.
For checked clusters, verify internal certificates once and check each use's boundary, formula substitution and scope against that certified interface. Cache entries must bind the actual definition, parameters, context and kernel version. The current ground-fragment controls are expensive: arithmetic and geometry response derivation take \(27.333\) and \(31.627\) seconds. This cost reflects millions of nodes in a low-level literal-machine expansion, not a measurement of simple marking equality. Compact logical checking, sparse marking checking and certified cluster reuse need separate timings.
Learned search-only markings can be erased for final proof checking. Logical markings that encode an inference rule are part of the validity system and must be checked, or replaced by a verified equivalent symbolic check. The next priority is economical direct logical interfaces and a measured checking-cost comparison; an uncolored polyomino realization remains an optional back end.


Propositional inference through distant marking receptors.

Its extended marking support checks those two formulas exactly and exposes \(B\) as the new line's result. Matching alone transmits values. The allowed inference family must also enforce the modus-ponens structure; arbitrary equal labels would not establish a derivation. Hypotheses and axiom schemas need their own checked origins. Use explicit locally checkable axiom and inference schemas so the verifier does not need to solve arbitrary tautology problems.
The attention analogy applies to proposing references: RL can choose which previous lines or certified clusters to connect. Proof connections use exact matching, not learned similarity weights. The complete candidate graph, global dead/forced checks, earliest-generation branching and primitive fallback remain required for a reference GCTS search. Marking support can reach earlier line ports even though those ports are already occupied and are outside the current frontier.
Canonical whole-formula IDs are a convenient bounded prototype representation. They do not by themselves realize one fixed finite literal palette: the eventual common base must carry fixed-alphabet formula encodings through syntax and routing clusters, checking complete strings or a certified canonical syntax DAG. Formula length and reference distance change cluster assemblies, not the base tile inventory. Generalization to quantifiers adds capture avoidance and scope interfaces.
This becomes the next direct proof-search design gate: compile and audit propositional rule families, discover fresh multi-step proofs using distant receptors, translate each inference to formulas and English, and compare the identical inventory against ordinary forward chaining/CSP and sparse symbolic checking. The next gate is a common family compiler with checked substitution, scope, boundary transport and recoverable primitive expansion.
