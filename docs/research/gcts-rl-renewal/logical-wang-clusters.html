Polyomino shapes can carry validity; markings can guide search.
This is a coherent alternative architecture. Compile the logical matching requirements into an uncolored, translation-only polyomino inventory and the encoded input/acceptance boundary. The final checker checks membership, allowed translations, complete cell coverage and the declared boundary. It ignores every learned search marking. A proved decoding theorem must ensure that every allowed completed tiling represents a derivation of the fixed statement.

Erase search markings
Check the unmarked tiling and decode its proof. Validity is independent of how the solution was found.
There is direct precedent: Golomb, Tiling with sets of polyominoes (1970) connects polyomino plane tiling to the Wang domino problem. Ollinger (2009) gives reductions using finitely many polyominoes, including a translation-only construction. Yang and Zhang (2024) explicitly encode Wang colors geometrically in a translation-only polyomino reduction. These establish a route for compiling computational matching into shape, not a practical proof-search advantage for our method.
Shape encodings need complementary boundary profiles and constraints forcing the intended macro alignment. A forward picture of interlocking pieces is insufficient: unintended offsets, reversed layouts and alternate decompositions must also be excluded or decoded. A plane-tiling undecidability theorem alone does not establish a fixed finite proof-certificate system with our chosen input and accepting boundaries. The finite-region compilation and its reverse decoding require their own proof.
Using markings only to order moves is compatible with complete primitive fallback and fair search. A redundant marking proved from the geometric system may prune safely. An unproved learned marking may restrict exploration, but failure under that marking remains unknown for the unmarked problem. It cannot license a claim that no proof exists. A successful tiling that passes the unmarked checker remains valid even if heuristic pruning was unsound.
Separating learned search from trusted checking is established practice in automated theorem proving; HOList / DeepHOL (2019) provides an RL environment built on HOL Light. This literature supports the general separation. It does not establish the particular combination of polyomino clusters and GCTS markings as a proven improvement.
Research judgment: keep the common first-order language and one base inventory as the architectural goal. Study an uncolored polyomino realization as an alternative back end. First demonstrate forward and reverse decoding on a small finite proof region, with a verifier that ignores search markings, then run matched marked/unmarked and classical-search controls. No polyomino proof compiler has been implemented in this notebook.


From ground fragments to reusable logical families.
A fragment here is tied to a concrete heap, proof context and physical layout. It is not yet a parameterized lemma family that can attach to any compatible receptor. The next gate is a common family compiler with checked substitution, scope, boundary transport and recoverable primitive expansion. Then a fresh arithmetic and Hilbert search must compose those families using the same fixed inventory, complete base fallback, global dead/forced checks, earliest-generation branching and exact rollback.
The polyomino proposal keeps learned markings outside the validity kernel. Its separate gate is a geometric realization whose unmarked finite tilings decode correctly in both directions. Neither representation currently establishes a distinctive GCTS benefit or net RL benefit. Compare identical inventories and charge compilation, discovery, validation and learning costs before making that claim. The broader tiling/proof research goal remains active.
