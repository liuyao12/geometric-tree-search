The turtle needs a nonconvex boundary

Why every convex corner fails

A bounded region tiled by finitely many whole turtles would be a polygon. At any genuine corner of a convex such polygon, at least one turtle has a vertex at the corner, and all its tiles lie inside the corner wedge. The two boundary rays come from tile edges. Cover a request with a movable boundary

From the certified plane tiling, take every tile whose interior meets the interior of the request \(K\). Their closed union \(U_{\mathrm{out}}\) covers \(K\). Keep only the tiles fully contained in \(K\) to form \(U_{\mathrm{in}}\). With turtle diameter \(D=\sqrt{48}\) in lattice units, these satisfy

\[ \{x\mid B_D(x)\subseteq K\}\subseteq U_{\mathrm{in}}\subseteq K \subseteq U_{\mathrm{out}}\subseteq K+B_D(0). \]

Every outer tile meets the request and has diameter \(D\). Every tile meeting a point at least \(D\) inside the request lies wholly inside it. The hierarchy prunes entire clusters whose supports miss the requested window.

Check the finite refutations with Wang squares

The geometry checker supplies clauses expressing the exhaustive root and hull-point domains. The same fixed Wang palette used for arithmetic and Hilbert geometry checks all 55 logical records for the seven representative wedges. A clause records that whenever the current tiles occur, some candidate from the next domain must occur. An empty domain closes that branch. The final record is the empty clause.
