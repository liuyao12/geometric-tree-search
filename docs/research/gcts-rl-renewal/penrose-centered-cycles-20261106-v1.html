Why the construction covers the plane

  1. Substitution preserves matching. Each rule is checked for exact containment, area and disjoint interiors. The two parent types agree on the subdivision word for each shared boundary label. This extends the check to every seam at every inflation. The coordinates and comparisons use \(\mathbb{Z}[\zeta_5]\) and its real subfield.

  2. The centered patch repeats. Let \(P\) be a recurring seed and \(\sigma\) the substitution. The center calculation proves \(P\subseteq\sigma^4(P)\) as marked patches. For the shorter return, set \(\tau=R_{-36^\circ}\circ\sigma^2\). Then \(P\subseteq\tau(P)\), and \(\tau^2(P)=\sigma^4(P)\) by the fivefold symmetry.

  3. The supports exhaust the plane. The initial union contains the disk of radius \(\tfrac14\) about the origin. The exact test checks each seed triangle’s altitude above its opposite edge. The support of \(\sigma^{4j}(P)\) contains the disk of radius \(\varphi^{4j}/4\). The nested limit is therefore a locally finite tiling. Every triangle diagonal eventually lies in the interior of a patch and matches the other half of a rhomb.

Why the resulting tiling has no translation period

The rhomb tiling retains the rotation \(R\). If \(p\) were a nonzero translation period, then \(Rp\) and \(R^{-1}p\) would also be periods. Their sum is a strictly shorter nonzero period.

\[ Rp+R^{-1}p=(\zeta_5+\zeta_5^{-1})p=(\varphi-1)p=\varphi^{-1}p. \]

Repeating this step produces periods of arbitrarily small positive length. The seed contains the origin as a rhomb vertex and has no other vertex in the disk of radius \(\tfrac14\). A period shorter than that would send the origin to another vertex in the disk, a contradiction.
