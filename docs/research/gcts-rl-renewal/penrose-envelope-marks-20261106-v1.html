Reduce a fixed-boundary failure to one directed edge

The earlier fixed hexagon gave two individually extendible seeds. This experiment tries each of its six oriented sides as the sole required edge. Two sides alone reproduce the contradiction. They yield the same template after rotation and translation.

Seed A is orange and seed B is lavender. The arrow is the required unit edge. The dashed green rhomb in each diagram is one legal way to cover it from the left. Each option conflicts with at least one seed.

For the oriented edge \(e\), let \(D(e)\) be its four possible rhombs. At least one must be present, and each member of \(D(e)\) has an independently checked incompatible contact with \(A\) or \(B\). Assuming both seeds leaves an empty domain. Unit propagation certifies \(\neg(A\land B)\) under the edge requirement.

On a new request the checker rebuilds the exact local domain in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]\), filters it to placements within the envelope, and replays the transported cut. The outside boundary of the selected patch is free inside the envelope. Full-sector targets and directed edge requirements stay fixed. Removing the edge or requiring the opposite side gives checked positive controls with both seeds.

Compare the same searches with and without the mark

Ten envelope tests and four connected seam tests use the same geometry checker and candidate sets across arms. Plain uses the initial GCTS order. Marked adds the transferred cuts. Learned adds the frozen policy trained on the earlier vertex-target cases. The two Glucose arms isolate the effect of giving the same cuts to a conventional solver.

The three negative envelope cases go from 3, 1 and 5 decisions to zero in GCTS. Marked Glucose also closes all three before branching. On the seven positive envelope cases and four positive seam cases the initial GCTS order uses the same decision counts with and without the mark. The learned order uses more decisions on all four seam cases. On the largest, it uses 512 decisions and 300 fresh cuts, compared with 206 decisions and zero fresh cuts for the initial order.

Attach alternative fillings to the same hexagon

Each footprint accepts either of two checked three-rhomb fillings. The requested vertex sectors and seed placements select allowed fillings. The recipes agree along every full hexagon edge, so choices that survive the local checks assemble along the seams. An independent global check tests every emitted witness for pairwise contact, containment and coverage of the requested receptors.
