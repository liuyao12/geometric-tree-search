For a finite first-order input, each well-sorted ground instance occurs at a finite position in the enumeration. Each admitted clause has finitely many predecessors in the queue. When the later of two clauses is processed, it can resolve against the earlier one. Duplicate clauses are represented by their first occurrence. The demand stage adds only finitely many candidates per round and uses a nesting cap of 24 for units that trigger new instances. That cap applies to guidance alone.

Herbrand’s theorem supplies a finite unsatisfiable set of ground instances for an unsatisfiable compiled clause set. Ground resolution eventually derives the empty clause from that finite set. This gives a semidecision procedure for first-order entailment in the unbounded algorithm. The implementation here runs finite experiments with Python’s resource limits.
