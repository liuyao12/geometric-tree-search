The exact guarded incidence problem.

\(\Gamma=\{H(P,Q,l,m),H(P,Q,m,n),\operatorname{Incident}(R,l)\},\qquad I.2,\Gamma\vdash\operatorname{Incident}(R,n)\)

The guard abbreviates the original predicates:

\(H(A,B,u,v)=\operatorname{Point}(A)\land\operatorname{Point}(B)\land\operatorname{Line}(u)\land\operatorname{Line}(v)\land\neg(A=B)\land\operatorname{Incident}(A,u)\land\operatorname{Incident}(B,u)\land\operatorname{Incident}(A,v)\land\operatorname{Incident}(B,v)\)

In words: the first two hypotheses describe distinct points incident with the indicated pairs of lines. The guarded uniqueness axiom identifies those lines. The third hypothesis says the indicated point is incident with the first line; the target asserts incidence with the last. These are abstract symbolic predicates, without drawn points, lengths or angles.

The claim is a ground sequent relative to all three hypotheses and the retained guarded incidence axiom. The designed decoder would retain the hypotheses as three named axioms in a flat shared-checker request. Neither recorded run produced a proof or that request. Such a request would preserve these assumptions rather than establish a universally closed theorem.
