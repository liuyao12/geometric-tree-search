CONTRIBUTION ASSESSMENT · SEPARATE STRUCTURE FROM SEARCH ADVANTAGE

Metatiles help. Contraction is not enough.

The complete metatile inventory is a working high-level proof interface: fragments discovered by earlier searches become legal candidates, retain their own formula and ownership markings, and expand into checked primitive derivations. Their absence never removes a primitive alternative. The nested-addition examples are proved from statements and finite bounds, without a human supplying their proof sequences.

Simply merging capacity points weakens the visible forcing information. Even with metatiles, coarse representations can spend their wall budget on branches that the fine representation avoids. Fewer frontier points do not imply faster proof search. This first contraction retains internal logical ports; it does not yet eliminate internal proof data through a boundary-only summary.

The classical control removes candidate values that lack support in another complete variable domain, then branches by minimum remaining values. It searches the identical primitive proof envelope with a different representation and scheduler. Its propagation work is counted and timed; zero assignment trials can still mean substantial work. Arc consistency is a classical constraint-search method; see Mackworth, Consistency in Networks of Relations (1977). This control establishes no theorem-specific advantage of GCTS over conventional constraint search.

Wang-style proof tiles remain the primary research track. The concrete contribution here is an automatically searched, checked, composable representation of proof fragments. A distinctive search advantage and useful RL acceleration remain unproved. The next useful test is an interface-preserving hierarchy on broader mathematical obligations, with a classical control that can also reuse the same checked fragments. Turtle and Penrose remain occasional geometric controls.

INSPECTABLE REPRESENTATION ARGUMENT · OWNERSHIP IS ESSENTIAL

Why the coarse model preserves solutions.

Partition the \(n\) primitive slots into consecutive groups \(G\) of size at most three. A candidate covering a distinct set of primitive slots \(E\) contributes

\[t_E(G)=\frac{|E\cap G|}{|G|}.\]

Integer twelfths represent these fractions exactly. A metatile retains the compatible union of all primitive output and premise \(m\)-values, including distant contacts. It also writes its own unique candidate identity at a separate ownership marking point for every slot it covers. Distinct candidate identities disagree on an overlapping slot, so their expansions cannot overlap. This channel defines the representation; it is not a learned failure marking.

  1. Ownership agreement makes the selected expansion sets disjoint.
  2. Exact coarse capacity gives \(\sum_E |E\cap G|=|G|\) for every group. Disjoint subsets with this total cover every primitive slot exactly once.
  3. The retained logical markings agree, so expansion yields an exact original primitive proof tiling.
  4. Every original solution lifts using its primitive candidates alone. Those candidates remain complete, and their disjoint ownership points never conflict.

Consequently, existence of an exact solution is equivalent in the two finite models. Without ownership, two half-capacity candidates could cover the same primitive slot and falsely fill a pair group. Tests include that counterexample and exhaustive equality of tiny expanded solution sets, both with and without metatiles.

An atomic metatile is justified by this solution-equivalence argument in the newly declared point model. It does not claim to reproduce the primitive scheduler's constituent path. The full bounded macro inventory is enumerated before search; a capped or sampled proposal pool is never used to declare a move forced. Candidate ordering is an authored preference for goal membership, member count and hierarchy level, with deterministic hash ties. There is no learned policy in this experiment.
