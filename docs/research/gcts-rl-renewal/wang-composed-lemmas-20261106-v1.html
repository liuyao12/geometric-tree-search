How one proof fragment becomes a family

The search first proves \(R(a)\) from \(P(a)\), \(\forall x\,(P(x)\Rightarrow Q(x))\) and \(\forall x\,(Q(x)\Rightarrow R(x))\). The local Wang verifier admits both resolution steps. A learner recognizes the two-step implication chain and replaces each whole ground atom with a slot. A receptor matches a unit or implication clause with the required signs and exact atom identities, keeping distinct slots distinct.

The eight-link arithmetic instance binds \(A_i\) to \(S^{8-i}(a)=S^{8-i}(b)\). In the four-link incidence instance the transport chain binds \(A_i\) to \(I(p,\ell_i)\).

The second donor proves \(R(a)\) from \(P(a)\), \(Q(a)\) and \(\forall x\,((P(x)\land Q(x))\Rightarrow R(x))\). The learner recognizes two removals from the same clause and preserves the residual clause as an open slot. Given previously admitted units \(B_1,\ldots,B_k\) and a clause \(C\) containing each \(\neg B_i\), a discharge call yields \(C\setminus\{\neg B_1,\ldots,\neg B_k\}\). Composition passes the residual clause to the next call. In the incidence proof a four-step discharge removes four of the five conditions for line uniqueness. An ordinary resolution removes the fifth, and a chain call helps transport incidence along equal lines.

The recognition patterns, receptor matching, and composition schedule were designed for this experiment. The two donor proofs and the receiver proofs were found by search. One translation-only square palette checks both domains. During search a successful recipe match supplies an instantiated proof fragment. The certificate expands that fragment into the original grounding and resolution language.
