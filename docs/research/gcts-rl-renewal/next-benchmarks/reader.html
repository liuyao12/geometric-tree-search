ONE SYMBOLIC LANGUAGE · A DECLARED INCIDENCE THEORY

Line identity followed by incidence transport.

The geometry is symbolic first-order logic. The one-sorted language has explicit point and line predicates and an incidence predicate. Its geometric axiom is Hilbert's guarded line uniqueness axiom, I.2. There are no coordinates, drawing-based inferences or implicit assumptions about the point \(C\).

The exact common-point guard \[\begin{aligned}H(A,B,l,m):={}&\operatorname{Point}(A)\land\operatorname{Point}(B)\land\operatorname{Line}(l)\land\operatorname{Line}(m)\\&\land\neg(A=B)\land\operatorname{Incident}(A,l)\land\operatorname{Incident}(B,l)\\&\land\operatorname{Incident}(A,m)\land\operatorname{Incident}(B,m).\end{aligned}\]

The literal syntax tree associates these conjunctions to the right. The guard names the full formula; it introduces no extra inference rule or axiom.

PRIMARY CANDIDATE · TWO GUARDED IDENTIFICATIONS

\[H(A,B,u,v),\ H(A,B,v,w),\ \operatorname{Incident}(C,u)\ \vdash_{\mathcal T_{\mathrm{I.2}}}\ \operatorname{Incident}(C,w).\]

In words: the distinct guarded points \(A\) and \(B\) lie on \(u\) and \(v\), and also on \(v\) and \(w\). If \(C\) lies on \(u\), show that \(C\) lies on \(w\).
