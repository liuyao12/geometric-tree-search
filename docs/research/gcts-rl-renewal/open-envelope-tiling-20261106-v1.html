Letting the tiles determine the outer boundary

Give the solver a core to cover, an envelope to stay inside, and one fixed seed tile. It returns a connected patch and computes its outside edges from the placements. The same search works with Turtles on \(A_2\) and unmarked edge-to-edge Penrose rhombs over \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]\).

The exact audit verified 40 positive runs. In 30 of those runs the patch covered a proper subset of the envelope. The earlier movable-boundary study supplied two candidate outlines per problem. These queries supply the core and envelope directly.

Connect a remote core to the seed

The green rings locate the small required cores. The search can place a tile over an uncovered interior point before it can reach that tile from the seed. Once the core is covered, it fills the missing connections by adding across the boundary of the seed component. The audit replayed both phases and checked the final connection graph.

A Turtle fallback pool enumerates lattice translations in the envelope's coordinate bounds. For Penrose rhombs the coordinate ring is dense in the plane. The fallback pool therefore grows by unit-edge attachments from the seed and stops at a depth given by an exact bound on the number of tiles.

What the search and the audit check

Let \(C\) be the union of the supplied convex core polygons, \(E\) the envelope, and \(U\) the union of the returned tiles. The certificate checks \(C\subseteq U\subseteq E\), disjoint tile interiors, and edge connectivity to the seed. For each core polygon \(C_i\), the verifier checks the exact area equation \[ A(C_i)=\sum_{t\in P} A(C_i\cap t), \] where \(P\) is the returned patch. The closed tiles cover the core once there is no positive-area gap. The independent audit computes intersections from contained vertices and edge crossings, then checks their convex hull areas. The search computes the remaining core by half-plane clipping.

At a forced edge, its midpoint lies in the interior of the core. The completed patch must cover both sides locally. The opposite tile must share the primitive edge in the Turtle model or the unit side in the Penrose model. The marking audit enumerates every such placement and checks its blocker in the receiving envelope.

When an uncovered interior point needs the fallback pool, the area bound \(N=\lfloor A(E)/\min_t A(t)\rfloor\) bounds the number of tiles. A seed-connected Penrose patch can reach each of its tiles in at most \(N-1\) full-edge steps. The pool enumerates placements within that depth and the envelope, using any tighter declared tile cap. The two pool-limit controls exhausted their budget and returned a bounded status. The tests concern finite seeded queries.
