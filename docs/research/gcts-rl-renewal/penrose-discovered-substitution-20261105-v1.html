The search recovered a subdivision of each unmarked half-rhomb. It then found compatible edge markings. One solution agrees with the standard arrowed Penrose rhombs and has a checked seed that can be nested to tile the whole plane.

Search from two shapes and a multiplier

Let \(\zeta=\exp(2\pi i/5)\), \(\rho=-\zeta^3\), and \(\varphi=-\zeta^2-\zeta^3\). The vertices are recorded as four integer coefficients in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta]\). The two input shapes have vertices \(A=(0,1,\rho)\) and \(B=(0,1,\rho^3)\). They are the halves of the thin and thick rhombs. The search received their geometry and the proposed multiplier \(\varphi\).

Candidate placements align a triangle vertex with a parent vertex or a vertex of an already placed child. Exact containment, disjointness and area equality found two geometric partitions for \(A\) and three for \(B\). The marking stage tried signed one- and two-arrow labels for unit edges and directed diagonal labels. It found 32 systems in this candidate space. The verifier independently aligns every parent boundary in the direction of its arrow. Edges of the same length and arrow type then have exactly the same child endpoints and arrow marks. Each of the four boundary prescriptions therefore composes across the corresponding seam at every subdivision.

A seed that grows to cover the plane

Let \(Q\) be the canonical marked \(A\) triangle. The highlighted copy is \(f(Q)\), where \(f(z)=\rho^8z+2+\zeta-\zeta^2-2\zeta^3\). Use the expansive similarity \(S=f^{-1}\circ(\varphi^4\,\cdot)\) to bring that copy back to \(Q\). Then \(Q\subset\operatorname{int}S(Q)\), and the transported patch on \(S(Q)\) contains the original marked tile.

Apply the same four-step refinement to the expanded tiles at each stage. Deterministic refinement and the verified seam prescriptions preserve the preceding patch. The fixed point of \(S\) lies inside \(Q\), so the nested supports contain disks with radii increasing by the factor \(\varphi^4\). They cover the plane. In the limit every diagonal has a matching mate. Joining each pair gives the two standard Penrose rhombs with matching unit-edge arrows.
