Learn from two geometric strips

Read each strip by lining up its Turtle placements with the earlier orientation stencil. Two tile orientations encode the letters. The two observations are \(\mathtt{0010}\) and \(\mathtt{00100010010}\). The generator reads the placements and the stencil from the finite assemblies.

Try simultaneous updates of two word registers \(A\) and \(B\). Each update concatenates a nonempty sequence of at most five register references. The first observed word initializes \(A\). To fit the second observation, the initial value of \(B\) must be a contiguous fragment of that observation. An update using only \(A\) would give a multiple of its length, which cannot turn four letters into eleven. The experiment enumerates every fitting rule within this grammar.

Promote each strip into its next receiver

The first receiver tries each predicted word and its reverse along the eligible seam lanes. It composes exact forbidden displacements, requires contact with the designated child roles, and checks that the assembled boundary is a single ring. The 18-, 22-, and 29-letter words each close a frame. Their triangles are all valid finite patches.

In the second receiver, the shorter-word branches leave two enclosed cavities in each of the twelve searched maximum-contact cores. Each cavity has area \(9/5\) times the area of a Turtle. A new whole tile with interior disjoint from the core must lie in one component of its complement. Filling a bounded component therefore requires an integer number of tile areas. This is an exact failure marking for any continuation that keeps the core in place.

The search starts with an empty mark bank. It learns the outline from the first failed 22-letter core, stores it up to lattice isometry, and uses it to skip eleven cap searches. Six skips transfer the same mark to the separate 18-letter branch.

The 29-letter branch admits the cap and the predicted 76-letter strips. The audit recomputes the rejected cavity boundaries from the actual core placements and checks nonoverlap and area for both the rejected cores and the completed patches.

Take the representative update \((A,B)\mapsto(ABA,AB)\), where juxtaposition means concatenation. Two successive applications of \((U,V)\mapsto(UV,U)\) give exactly that update. With \((A,B)=(\mathtt{0010},\mathtt{001})\), it is the even-step subsequence of the strip rule in the existing plane certificate.

Two alternatives simply store \(BA\) or \(AB\) in their second register. For the remaining alternative, write its auxiliary word as \(C\). The initial words satisfy \(AC=BA\). This identity gives the same next visible word \(AAC=ABA\) and propagates because \(A'C'=ABAAC=ABABA=B'A'\). Thus all four rules agree indefinitely on the output, including the two shortest descriptions under the experiment’s rule cost.

The recovered strip sequence is therefore interchangeable with the strip sequence in the earlier coordinate recurrence at every level.
