Exact tiling with fixed and movable boundaries

One search engine tiled finite regions with unmarked Turtles on \(A_2\) and edge-to-edge unit Penrose rhombs over \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]\). It grew the cover from the specified outline, using both individual tiles and clusters learned from smaller outlines. The tests included concave boundaries, holes, and a choice between two outer boundaries.

On the two largest cases, the cluster arm reduced placement checks from 7,752 to 1,909 for Turtle and from 956 to 371 for Penrose. Both complete covers passed an independent exact check.

Two larger covers built from smaller clusters

The cluster inventory comes from solving three smaller boundary problems for each tile system, starting with single tiles. Each learned cluster carries its own oriented boundary and can be moved to a different attachment. In these figures, solid outlines distinguish clusters and white seams show their constituent tiles.

A candidate cluster must match the chosen exposed edge and at least one further edge of the current frontier. The inverse receptor index identifies which templates can meet the second edge. The search ranks those candidates, tries at most five cluster offers for that frontier choice, and retains the complete single-tile inventory. This threshold was added after the initial pilot exposed the cost of testing too many weakly aligned clusters.

Choose an outer boundary around a shared core

Each family allows two exact outer outlines. Both contain the blue core tile. The search evaluates the obstructed alternative first, then returns the feasible one. These tests use finite menus of permitted outlines. The test outlines were designed to exercise local obstruction transfer.

Each donor mark lists an exact blocker for every tile that could cover its red edge. The receiving boundary adds a tile-shaped extension elsewhere and translates the outline. The solver carries the donor's blockers to the new edge and checks them there. The marked search rejected both obstructed outlines before enumerating placements.

On each training episode, a contextual bandit chose one order and received a reward based on the placement checks and nodes used to solve the region. The orders favor larger clusters, more sealed frontier edges, or single tiles first. Each policy trained on 15 small outlines per tile system. The size-aware version also saw 15 larger outlines per system and kept separate estimates above a 20-tile threshold.

The small-region policy chose singles first on the largest Turtle region and took 24,416 placement checks. The size-aware follow-up chose the same order as the cluster baseline there, taking 1,909 checks. On the larger Penrose region, the size-aware policy prioritized sealed edges and used 365 checks compared with 371 for largest-first. These are outcomes on the held-out outlines in this notebook.

What the exact certificates establish

The input is one counterclockwise polygonal outline with any holes listed clockwise. A search move attaches to a directed segment of the residual boundary. The Turtle adapter splits sides into primitive lattice segments in the admitted directions. The Penrose adapter splits sides into unit edges. In either model a full tiling of a residual region must contain a tile on the interior side of each exposed segment.

The Turtle adapter checks occupancy in the exact cells of the lattice arrangement. For the Penrose adapter, the signed cross product, divided by \(\sin(\pi/5)\), has the form \(a+b\varphi\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2\). The adapter checks containment by winding and exact clipping against each convex rhomb. It also checks pairwise edge-to-edge contact.

A cover certificate adds the oriented boundaries of the placed tiles and cancels opposite shared segments. The resulting chain equals the requested region boundary. For any point off the edges, the sum of tile winding numbers equals the region winding number. Its values are one in the region and zero outside. This certifies coverage with multiplicity one, including the holes. The audit also checks pairwise geometry independently.

A rejection mark certifies that one required edge has no legal placement. It names an outside sample, a boundary segment that intrudes into a rhomb, or an incompatible already placed tile for each candidate. Rechecking those guards at a new edge proves the same exclusion there. Single-tile branches remain available whenever no certificate rules them out. The search uses a declared node cap for each query.

The audit checked 50 positive search arms and replayed 335 applied failure markings. These are finite-region results. The plane-tiling certificates are in the separate substitution notebooks.
