What helps search find a Wang proof?

Changing the order of local rewrites gave mixed results. A second experiment grouped checked distributivity and rearrangement lemmas into a move that connected the two expressions through a common form. It proved all 40 held-out equalities.

What the controller learned

All schedulers used the same bidirectional term graph, exact rewrite generator and inventory of 11 earlier theorems. A search step expanded a term nominated by a breadth, balanced or target-directed queue. Intermediate terms were capped at eight nodes beyond the larger endpoint. The controller observed elapsed work, the endpoint shapes, best progress toward either endpoint and the number of distinct available nominations. A successful connection earned 15, a continued search cost one and a training timeout cost 16. A temporal-difference SARSA update learned which queue to select. Sutton and Barto, Chapter 6

The three models trained on 36 equations from four families for 12 rounds each. The strongest learned seed and the strongest fixed queue were chosen on 16 equations from two calibration families. All settings were frozen for 40 equations in four other families. The split excludes identical formula pairs under variable renaming or reversal. Formula structures within each family are related. This is a small transfer study in one arithmetic signature.

The inventory stops at multiplication associativity. It withholds the binomial product and the square-of-a-sum theorem. Every returned proof path was checked against the exact initial and final terms. Four polynomially unequal controls yielded zero checked proofs.

Compose a move across both boundaries

The local search failure motivated a separate follow-up. First distribute each product over its sums. Then associate and sort factors within a product and summands within a sum. The common expression is a boundary shared by the two proof clusters. Each side produces a trace of exact rewrites, and concatenating one trace with the other in reverse proves the equation.
