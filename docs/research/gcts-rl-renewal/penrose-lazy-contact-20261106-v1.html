Tile a specified polygon

ThinThickRequired seed

Vertices have exact coordinates in \(\mathbb Z[\zeta_5]\). The tiles are the two unmarked Penrose rhombs with unit sides, and contacts must be edge to edge. In units of \(\sin(\pi/5)\), polygon area has the form \(a+b\varphi\), where \(\varphi=(1+\sqrt{5})/2\). A thin rhomb contributes one unit and a thick rhomb contributes \(\varphi\), so the two coefficients uniquely fix the tile counts.

For a convex polygon with \(N\) tiles, every tile vertex is reachable from a boundary corner in at most \(2N+1\) unit steps. The first rhomb has four vertices, and each rhomb added across a full edge introduces at most two. Enumeration within that radius gives a finite placement inventory. The solver enforces the tile counts and covers each unit boundary edge exactly once. An independent check verifies containment, pairwise contact and exact total area. These conditions imply full coverage.

Learn from failed local contacts

Each target requires its ten angular sectors to be covered exactly once. The SAT solver proposes a selection of rhombs. Exact geometry checks each selected pair and turns any invalid contact into a reusable forbidden pair. Donor certificates supply the warm marks. Withholding complete rotation-and-translation orbits keeps the four test seed obstructions out of this donor inventory.

Four separate training fixtures supplied sixteen episodes for the frozen reinforcement learning policy. Plain starts without donor marks. Guarded uses the marks with the initial move order. Learned and random change that order. Glucose uses the same warm clauses and adds exact cuts in the same outer loop.

All five arms settled the same eight positives and five negatives. For the slit, warm marks reduced the number of fresh geometric cuts from 310 to 80, and the learned order needed zero. Glucose also needed zero and ran faster on this case. The learned order required more decisions than the initial order in seven of the eight positive cases. Decision counts across solvers reflect different branching implementations.

The earlier unmarked geometric depth-first search exhausted its budget on the slit and on a second positive target. The new solver produces checked tilings for both.
