Objects and relations, with no geometric oracle.

The theory contains the three planar incidence axioms from Hilbert’s second edition (1903), section 2. The spatial axioms are outside this plane fragment. Point and line are explicit sorts in a one-sorted logical presentation; incidence is an uninterpreted binary relation. Equality is logical equality.

\[\operatorname{Point}(A),\qquad \operatorname{Line}(\ell),\qquad \operatorname{Inc}(A,\ell).\]

The sort presentation makes every object a point or a line, keeps those sorts disjoint, and types incidence. These are displayed separately from the source axioms. The phrase “determine a line” in the first two axioms is expressed by existence and uniqueness. The third axiom has two clauses: two distinct points on each line, and three noncollinear points in the plane.

No distance, angle, intersection routine, coordinates or drawing contributes a fact. Renaming the three relation names preserves the certificate check. The proof tiles have their own coordinates solely to locate proof cells and marking contacts.

Actual search, and its limits.
GCTS automatically discovers both proofs from the complete declared finite candidate inventory. The classical arc-consistency control also finds both, with fewer search states. This establishes checkable automatic proof selection in a Hilbert incidence fragment; it supplies no distinctive GCTS advantage.


Every solver rebuilds its catalog from the theory, target and finite bounds. No human proof sequence, earlier certificate, learned policy or finite model is input. The compiler authors the logical rule types: every sorted outer instantiation, every orientation of every propositional clause, and the declared atomic equality templates. A complete backward dependency cone retains cycles. Grouping a conjunction and expanding an axiom are authored proof blocks, rather than learned mathematical lemmas.


Both controls use the same point inventory, resource certificate and cell bound. GCTS checks global dead ends, global forced moves, then the earliest generation; every finite root obligation has generation zero. The classical control uses arc consistency and minimum-domain branching. One cold run per solver and control is shown. Catalog construction, native verification and primitive replay are included in total cold time; generic native compilation and the subsequent full audit are additional costs.


The longer incidence proof costs substantially more to check than to search: the unchanged native checker exhaustively evaluates propositional truth assignments. An initial native gate reached its step budget; the complete matrix was rerun with a fixed larger limit. Every positive record shown here has complete native acceptance. Shortened strips, the wrong statement and removal of uniqueness exhaust this finite grammar; such search failures alone do not prove nonderivability in unrestricted logic.


Independent semantic controls.
A finite abstract incidence model satisfies all the displayed axioms, supports the two theorem statements, and falsifies the deliberately wrong assertion. The second interpretation adds a line through two points of an existing three-point line. It preserves every displayed axiom except line uniqueness and falsifies both theorem statements. These give concrete countermodels for the weakened foundation, independently of finite search exhaustion.
These are models of incidence only. They are not Hilbert planes with order and congruence. Their finite membership tables never enter proof search.
