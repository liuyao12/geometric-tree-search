Share equality and demand an instance

Keep the searchThe persistent engine retains its growing term stream and FIFO resolution queue. It also schedules useful instances and inferences from the current goal and clauses.
Share equal termsA proof-producing congruence graph groups equal terms, propagates equality through functions and explains every use with ordinary grounding and resolution records.
Match a constructorA compound term already present can match a pattern extracted from a formula schema. The matching substitution proposes an actual instance of that schema.

For example, the successor-left proof used the observed term \(\left(S(a)+b\right)\) to match the extracted pattern \(\left(\mathrm{v1}+\mathrm{v1\_0}\right)\). This yielded the substitution

  • \(\mathrm{v1}\mapsto S(a)\)
  • \(\mathrm{v1\_0}\mapsto b\)

Matching examines one queued term per round. For variables not fixed by a match it tries at most sixteen combinations drawn from the first and last three visible terms of each required sort. This is a bounded proposal mechanism. The underlying enumeration remains available.

Congruence closure is an established automated reasoning method. Nelson and Oppen’s primary paper describes its use for equality reasoning. This experiment integrates the earlier proof-producing equality implementation into the persistent search. The measured improvement is evidence for that integration. It does not establish an advantage of geometric search over conventional equality reasoning.

The returned records are independently expanded into the shared Wang verifier palette and the explicit long-range copy rule. The preceding notebook explains the colored copy markings. Source markings anchor each schema to the translated hypotheses and denied goal. Parent markings copy complete clauses from earlier records. Native local rectangles check each instance or resolution. The equality graph and scheduling policy are absent from this final validity check.

With both additions the two step proofs contain thirteen and twenty-four primitive records. On the zero-left step, learned seed 47 reduces resolution attempts from 1,147 to 227. Its other work also changes, so resolution counts are not a wall-clock speedup measurement. The graph work above is charged separately and should be included when assessing cost.

The harder symbolic Hilbert betweenness case remains open at its resolution limit in all seven configurations. The control asking for zero-left addition from recursion alone also remains open. Its two-element countermodel shows why an induction principle is needed for that supplied axiom set.

The useful outcome is automated discovery of the missing arithmetic step proofs, followed by marked-tile checking. These experiments have not compared performance with a modern external prover, discovered new mathematics or demonstrated learned reusable theorem clusters.
