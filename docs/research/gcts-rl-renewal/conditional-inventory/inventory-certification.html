Assessment and next experiment

This is useful checker and inventory engineering evidence. It establishes neither a new GCTS search advantage nor an RL gain: the larger inputs never reach either search method, and no new RL training occurs. The two methods retain their existing semantics: complete point-candidate GCTS scheduling versus a specialized chronological symbolic control.

The next certification experiment should group several definitions into dependency-closed batches, retaining every definition and the same aggregate allowance. Batch planning or adaptive splits must charge all setup and failed probes, keep all-or-none admission, and retain a fresh final whole-proof Native gate. Its capacity and cost remain unmeasured. Single-definition checking is an explicit tested control.

The separate recursive-cluster design proposes a bounded missing-premise DAG with typed open receptors, shared row identities and scheduler-validated continuations. It remains a design, with no implementation or new RL/proof-search result. Once full inventories can be certified practically, it should be tested as a separate factor against the same conventional support heuristic.

Source reviews and saved-data audits have distinct scopes and disclosed inherited authorship. Parallel agents review registry/driver composition, plans, mathematical data and resources; Root freezes the experiments, performs separate admission and publishes. All timed runs were sequential with other agents idle. Native checker correctness remains an inherited premise, rather than a newly proved kernel theorem.

The research program remains active: a growing learned lemma hierarchy, a fixed universal proof-tile palette, Peano induction, Hilbert geometry, unmarked Turtle/Penrose experiments and infinite-plane or substitution certificates are not established by this experiment.
