One machine is frozen before any theorem instance

Fix a finite tape alphabet \(\Gamma\), a finite state set \(Q\), an initial state \(q_0\), an accepting state \(q_{\rm acc}\), and one partial deterministic table

\[\delta:Q\times\Gamma\rightharpoonup Q\times\Gamma\times\{-1,0,+1\}.\]

A transition writes its new symbol, changes state and then moves by its displacement. The accepting state has no outgoing transition. An undefined transition at any other state is a nonaccepting halt. Plain cells and head cells have the disjoint alphabet \(A=\Gamma\sqcup(Q\times\Gamma)\). The wall symbol \(\#\) lies outside \(A\).

For a strip of width \(W\ge1\), a valid configuration is a word in \(A^W\) with exactly one head. The only external neighbors are \(\#\) at positions \(-1\) and \(W\). Every future theorem instance must use the same alphabet, table, tile prototypes, source-kernel policy and color coding. The actual full first-order checker table is absent from this artifact.

The local update is partial on purpose

For \(a,c\in A\cup\{\#\}\) and \(b\in A\), define \(F(a,b,c)\) by these exhaustive cases. First declare it undefined when the triple contains two heads, when its head has no transition, or when a head at \(b\) moves into a neighboring wall.

Remaining caseOutput at the center
No head in the tripleKeep the plain symbol \(b\).
Head at \(b=(q,s)\), with \(\delta(q,s)=(q',s',0)\)Write \((q',s')\).
Head at \(b\), moving to a present neighborWrite plain \(s'\).
Head at \(a\) moves right, or head at \(c\) moves left; \(b=z\in\Gamma\)Write \((q',z)\).
A neighboring head does not enter the centerKeep plain \(b\).

The wall test is essential: without it, a head could leave the strip and disappear, producing a spurious headless row. Halting rows also have no computation successor; acceptance is handled by a separate boundary layer.

Overlapping pairs force the old neighborhood

For every triple on which \(F\) is defined, include one tile \(T(a,b,c)\). Its north and south colors are cell values; its west and east colors are ordered pairs. Color constructors are tagged so pairs, collector flags, initial-language states and the stop color cannot accidentally match.

\(N=b\)\(S=F(a,b,c)\)\(W=(a,b)\)\(E=(b,c)\)\(T(a,b,c)\)
\(N=c\)\(S=F(b,c,d)\)\(W=(b,c)\)\(E=(c,d)\)\(T(b,c,d)\)
The shared pair \((b,c)\) fixes both overlapping old cells.

The side relation for a computation row requires a left pair \((\#,b_0)\) and a right pair \((b_{W-1},\#)\). Internal walls are impossible because a pair containing a wall could only match a tile whose north center is a wall, while every north center belongs to \(A\). There are finitely many tile prototypes, bounded by \(|A|(|A|+1)^2\), for each frozen table.

A collector requires an accepting head

The final layer reads cell colors from above and puts the stop color \(\bot_{\rm stop}\) below. A plain cell has a collector for each unchanged flag \(0\to0\) or \(1\to1\). An accepting head has exactly the flag change \(0\to1\). No collector reads a nonaccepting head, changes \(1\to0\), or counts an accepting head after the flag is already one.

Its west boundary is \(\mathrm{seen}(0)\), its east boundary is \(\mathrm{seen}(1)\), and every south boundary color is \(\bot_{\rm stop}\). Pair and seen colors are disjoint, so a matched row cannot mix computation and collector tiles. No north edge accepts the stop color, so a collector cannot occur above a further bulk row.

Headless is not accepting. An all-plain bottom keeps a constant flag and cannot connect the required left zero to right one. Two accepting heads also fail at the second flag change.

The finite-strip lemma

Conditional statement. For a fixed valid one-head initial row \(C_0\in A^W\), a complete rectangle with \(H\) computation rows, one collector row, top \(C_0\), the stated side relations and a stop bottom exists exactly when the machine has an in-strip run of \(H\) steps from \(C_0\) to an accepting configuration. Here \(H\ge0\); a zero-step rectangle accepts only an already accepting initial row.
  1. Recover each triple. Write the tiles in any computation row as \(T(a_x,b_x,c_x)\). Horizontal matching gives \((b_x,c_x)=(a_{x+1},b_{x+1})\), hence \(a_x=b_{x-1}\) and \(c_x=b_{x+1}\) internally. The two side relations provide the wall neighbors. This also covers \(W=1\), when both neighbors are walls.
  2. Identify one machine step. Suppose the north word has its unique head at \(j\). Every center farther than one position from \(j\) keeps its plain symbol. The center at \(j\) receives the written symbol and either retains the new head for a stay move or becomes plain. Exactly the destination neighbor receives the new head for a moving step. These are precisely the cases defining \(F\). A halt or attempted escape makes the head-centered tile unavailable. Thus an existing row represents one legal in-strip step and its south word again has exactly one head.
  3. Compose soundly. Vertical matching equates each south word with the next north word. Induction over the \(H\) rows therefore recovers an actual run from the specified \(C_0\). The independent collector flag argument forces the final head state to be \(q_{\rm acc}\); no headless or nonaccepting final word passes.
  4. Construct every genuine finite run. Given such a run, choose at every position its actual old triple. The step stays inside the strip and is nonhalting before its final configuration, so every required \(F\) value exists. Pair and vertical colors match by construction. Put flag zero before the final accepting head and flag one afterward; the collector then matches both boundaries. This proves completeness.

This standard computation-tableau compilation argument received independent prose review: 51 checks, no blocking finding for the conditional strip lemma. It is not machine checked. The lemma quantifies over a specified finite table; it does not supply the missing proof-checker table or establish an implemented universal tile set.

The certificate is an unknown boundary word

A fixed four-state regular grammar can select a well-formed initial row of the form

\[\square^{\ell}\,(q_0,\mathrm{START})\,u\,\mathrm{SEP}\,v\,\mathrm{END}\,\square^r.\]

The public binary word \(u\) encodes the original statement, its declared context and frozen theory profile. The binary certificate word \(v\) remains unknown. The grammar has the following phases; only the first transition creates a head.

The transitions are \(L\xrightarrow{\square}L\), \(L\xrightarrow{(q_0,\mathrm{START})}U\), loops on plain binary bits at \(U\) and \(P\), \(U\xrightarrow{\mathrm{SEP}}P\), \(P\xrightarrow{\mathrm{END}}R\), and \(R\xrightarrow{\square}R\). The left state is \(L\) and the required final state is \(R\). A top selector for each transition \(p\xrightarrow{b}p'\) carries west/east colors \(\mathrm{init}(p),\mathrm{init}(p')\) and south color \(b\).

For a chosen left padding \(\ell\), public point markings pin the head/start cell, every bit of \(u\), and its immediately following separator. The proof bits, their end position and right padding remain choices. A finite alphabet can represent arbitrarily long finite formulas and proofs through unbounded word length; no per-theorem tile grammar is introduced. The lossless source/proof codecs and actual checker interpretation of these words remain missing.

Exact point values implement the edges

Use \(L=\mathbb Z^2\), translations \(G=2\mathbb Z^2\), and no rotations or reflections. Every prototype contributes \(t(0,0)=1\) and zero elsewhere. It assigns edge markings at the four midpoint offsets. Channel \(V\) carries north/south colors, \(H\) carries west/east colors, and \(R\) carries a center role.

Adjacent centers differ by two, so they share exactly their relevant midpoint. Unit occupancy selects one tile at each required center; matching midpoint values are exactly edge matching. Color codes must be injective exact integers. An assigned zero flag is a value; a missing component is \(*\).

The required bulk centers are \((2x,2y)\) for \(0\le x<W\), \(0\le y\le H\). Add left/right selector centers at \((-2,2y)\), \((2W,2y)\); top/bottom centers at \((2x,-2)\), \((2x,2H+2)\); and top-corner centers at \((-2,-2)\), \((2W,-2)\). Every one of these centers is a required unit-capacity point, and admissible origins are exactly this finite target.

Every prototype has an assigned center role. At its center, every core tile and every collector tile assigns channel \(R\) the value bulk. Every selector assigns its exact declared role: left, right, top-fixed, top-grammar, bottom, init-left or init-right. No prototype may omit its center role or use a wildcard role to bypass the fixed background. A future literal inventory must serialize all of these assignments; that inventory remains absent.

A fixed background role marking admits only bulk, left, right, top-fixed, top-grammar, bottom, initial-left or initial-right prototypes at their corresponding centers. Left/right selectors expose the finite pair-or-seen relations toward the bulk; bottom selectors expose stop; top-fixed selectors expose only their chosen cell color; top-grammar selectors expose cell and grammar-state colors; the two top-corner selectors expose initial states \(L\) and \(R\). Other selector components are absent. Roles and public-word pins define the requested instance and are not learned acceleration constraints.

Both top-selector families are finite and belong to the same fixed inventory. In top-fixed mode, every shared south color is pinned to the specified \(C_0\), so the lemma applies to an arbitrary valid initial row. In top-grammar mode, distinct role marks exclude top-fixed selectors and enforce the unknown-certificate language. The top-corner flags add no constraint in top-fixed mode because its horizontal components are absent.

Consequently every exact point solution restricts to a rectangle satisfying the stated boundary relations in its chosen top mode, and every such rectangle extends to the matching selectors. This is a finite point problem with explicit background and boundary data. It does not yet give a uniform-plane Wang boundary gadget, a serialized fixed-set artifact or an infinite tiling certificate.

What the foundational result does—and what remains

Before a universal first-order system claim, freeze one source-theory/kernel policy \(\mathcal P_*\), one total syntactic checker \(\operatorname{Check}_{\mathcal P_*}(u,v)\), and one finite machine \(M_*\) implementing it. The checker must handle full finite formula/term encodings, equality, quantifier/eigenvariable and capture conditions. Source assumptions must be explicit and identical across lanes. A full PA profile needs a generic schema recognizer; the corrected Hilbert dependent context requires its own faithful interpretation. These objects are requirements here, not artifacts already supplied.

Enumerating \((W,H,\ell)\) by increasing \(W+H+\ell\), with integer lexicographic ties and complete finite-board enumeration, is effective and fair. Every accepted finite certificate has a finite accepting run and visits finitely many tape cells, so sufficient bounds and padding eventually occur. An implementation whose subsearches can remain unknown must dovetail them. A fixed bound or cutoff cannot establish non-theoremhood.

Even if checking each candidate halts, searching all candidates need not halt on a non-theorem. The general first-order decision problem is undecidable; this background comes from the original Church note and Turing paper. Turing also supplies the historical universal-machine framework; the present artifact neither adopts his table nor claims his exact original problem was phrased as the modern halting problem.

The expressive bridge is still missing. A high-level logical proof step or learned cluster needs a checkable expansion into this frozen core and an interface certificate: input/output tape sections, head state and position, boundaries, resource span and marking version. Reusable lemmas need source proofs and exact scopes. Macro composition, independent source decoding, and the practical reusable FOL receptor inventory remain unimplemented. The raw tableau does not imply that search becomes efficient.
