What GCTS contributes here

The system does discover checkable target proofs without a human supplying their row sequence. Its rules and generic lemma templates are programmed, and automatic specialization supplies a case-specific inventory. Root independently checks the retained complete point region and exact final Native request, including theory, target, proof word and used lemma bodies.

This cohort establishes no solve-rate advantage for GCTS or the shared heuristic. Conventional symbolic search solves exactly the same cases. On the three primary search failures, the shared helper still finds no proof; its scoring work is charged to the same search budget. The earlier curried success remains useful development evidence, but its benefit does not transfer to these harder cases under the current grammar and budgets.

A narrower improvement appears in proof quality: for equality transport and its correlated renaming, shared support reduces the GCTS target-essential primitive expansion from \(9\) commands at depth \(5\) to \(5\) commands at depth \(3\). Both symbolic orderings already obtain the shorter argument. Successful GCTS placement counts also decrease; the solved-case set remains unchanged.

The geometric method maintains the full candidate/frontier graph with global dead-end and forced-move propagation, earliest-generation branching and exact rollback. The symbolic control uses chronological prefix search and is a specialized conventional control. It is not a state-of-the-art external prover. Matching this control is a useful diagnostic milestone; comparison with existing proof-search systems remains open.

There is no new RL training in this cohort. Reusable level-one lemma families are present, but recursive learning of a growing hierarchy of new lemmas remains unestablished. These are first-order statements, with no new Peano arithmetic or Hilbert geometry result. A fixed universal Wang palette, Turtle/Penrose tiling results and plane-tiling proofs remain separate open goals.
