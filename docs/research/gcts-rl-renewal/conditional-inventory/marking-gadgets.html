What makes the translation exact
The allowed-origin set is exactly the original required centers plus the new auxiliary centers. Every prototype assigns a center anchor. Anchor pins force a base lift at an original center and the correct auxiliary role at each added center. A fresh choice channel records the literal base ID. Separate state points connect the auxiliary steps.


For a nonempty clause of length \(k\), the emitted family contains \(2k|T|\) auxiliary prototypes and \(k\) additional occupied centers. An empty clause adds an impossible anchored center with no matching prototype. Multiple clauses share the base choice channel but have separate state points and auxiliary roles.


The independent verifier reconstructs every expected prototype, pin, role and origin from the literal input and validated layout. It also checks the entire translated point-by-channel assignment union. Mark-only points and explicitly assigned zero remain constraints; missing channels remain free. Projection recovers literal base IDs, including geometrically identical prototypes with different IDs.
