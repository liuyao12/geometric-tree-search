What finite equality markings can represent

Provenance and scope. This is an elementary mathematical proof derived by the agent, not a proof discovered or checked by the experimental search engine. It belongs to mathematical analysis, outside automated-discovery and benchmark counts. No experimental data, private implementation, learned model or runtime proof object was used. The exact pair representation is possible under translation-only, fixed-type, distinct-placement semantics. It does not represent arbitrary higher-order exclusions, prove complete tilability, or imply useful search speed.

Model and statement

Let \(L\cong\mathbb Z^r\) be a discrete additive lattice and \(I\) a finite set of prototype types. Fix exact finite-support base functions \(t_i:L\to[0,1]\). A placement is \((i,x)\), and each placement identity is selected at most once. For a finite set \(S\), write

\[T_S(p)=\sum_{(i,x)\in S}t_i(p-x).\]

Base partial capacity legality means \(T_S(p)\le1\) everywhere. A channel marking \(m_{i,\gamma}:D_{i,\gamma}\to\{0,1\}\) is a coherent partial function with finite domain. Its translated value at \(x+u\) is \(m_{i,\gamma}(u)\). Only values assigned at the same point and channel are compared. Missing is unconstrained; assigned \(0\) is a value. The base data stay fixed.

A finite set \(F\subset I\times I\times L\) forbids distinct placements \((i,x)\ne(j,y)\) when \((i,j,y-x)\in F\). A descriptor denotes every admitted translation of its pair. The descriptions \((i,j,d)\) and \((j,i,-d)\) denote the same unordered physical exclusion.

Representation theorem. There are finitely many binary equality channels and finite marking supports such that, for every finite placement set,

\[ \operatorname{Legal}_{t,m}(S) \iff [\,\forall p,\ T_S(p)\le1\,]\ \land\ [\,\nexists (i,x)\ne(j,y)\in S:\ (i,j,y-x)\in F\,]. \]

This is an equivalence for partial legality. It makes no assertion that such a partial state extends, fills a target, or tiles an infinite domain.

Construction and complete proof

Close the descriptors under \((i,j,d)\mapsto(j,i,-d)\), discard the vacuous \((i,i,0)\) descriptors, and choose one representative per remaining reciprocal orbit. Call the finite representative set \(R\) and put \(q=|R|\). For each \(f=(A,B,d)\in R\), make one private channel \(\gamma_f\) with just

\[ m_{A,\gamma_f}(0)=0,\qquad m_{B,\gamma_f}(-d)=1. \]

Leave every other slot in that channel unassigned. If \(A=B\), both entries belong to that one prototype, and \(d\ne0\) makes their domain points distinct.

  1. If \(A\ne B\), an \(A\)-placement at \(x\) assigns \(0\) at \(x\), while a \(B\)-placement at \(y\) assigns \(1\) at \(y-d\). A disagreement occurs exactly when \(x=y-d\), or \(y-x=d\). Placements of the same type assign only equal values in this channel. Other types assign nothing. Thus this channel rejects exactly its declared pair orbit.

  2. If \(A=B\), each placement at \(x\) assigns \(0\) at \(x\) and \(1\) at \(x-d\). It is internally coherent. Distinct translations \(x,y\) can disagree only through \(x=y-d\) or \(x-d=y\); these give \(y-x=d\) or \(y-x=-d\). These are precisely the same unordered same-type exclusion. Equal-value overlaps do not reject anything.

  3. The cross-type argument also covers \(d=0\): different types are rejected exactly at equal translations. The same-type case \(d=0\) is vacuous for two distinct placements and is omitted. The literal double assignment there would not be a function.

  4. Every forbidden pair therefore creates a disagreement in its representative channel. Conversely, a marking inconsistency has two unequal values at one point and channel. Coherence of each prototype implies that two distinct placements supplied them; the preceding analysis identifies a declared pair orbit. Different private channels never compare with one another, even if their spatial supports overlap. Hence the conjunction creates exactly the desired exclusions.

  5. The unchanged capacity condition conjoined with this marking equivalence is exactly the theorem. There are \(q\) channels and \(2q\) assigned prototype-channel-point slots. The supports are finite, but may lie far outside positive base support. This is a representation-size bound, not an optimality or runtime bound.

The precise caveat. No counterexample exists under the stated distinct-pair assumptions. If \((A,A,0)\) instead means “ban even one \(A\)-placement,” the assertion is false for coherent equality markings: a base-legal singleton cannot disagree with another placement. For example, \(t_A=\mathbf1_{\{0\}}\) on \(L=\mathbb Z\) has a legal singleton. Likewise, an order-sensitive relation on a sequence is not legality of a placement set. The minus sign in the construction matters: it produces \(y-x=d\), not the opposite cross-type offset.

The converse exposes unwanted pair exclusions

Every fixed finite-channel, finite-support equality marking also has an exact finite effective pair list:

\[ F_m=\{(i,j,u-v): \exists\gamma,\ u\in D_{i,\gamma},\ v\in D_{j,\gamma},\ m_{i,\gamma}(u)\ne m_{j,\gamma}(v)\}. \]

The list is finite. Two translated assignments meet exactly when \(x+u=y+v\), equivalently \(y-x=u-v\); unequal values then give precisely a pair in this list. A coherent prototype cannot generate a same-type zero-offset conflict. Thus finite fixed equality markings describe exactly finite translation-invariant pair exclusions, relative to the unchanged capacity rule. Compressing or fitting markings can create extra difference offsets; preserving sampled contacts alone does not prove those additional exclusions sound.

Conditional corollary: certified pairs give redundant markings

Fix the same original base model and context \(\mathcal C\), including admitted placements, required points, boundary or exterior data, seeds, inventory and solution meaning. Suppose every pair in the finite list, at every admitted translation, is separately proved impossible in any original exact solution.

Then the constructed markings preserve exactly the solution set in \(\mathcal C\). Every original solution avoids the proved-impossible pairs, and the construction rejects no unlisted pair, so its markings agree. Conversely, forgetting the added markings leaves the identical original base problem. For an infinite solution whose original pointwise conditions are already well-defined, any marking conflict would still be witnessed by two placements, giving the same argument. This is equality of solution sets, possibly empty; it proves neither existence nor infinite extension.

Capacity-legal partial states containing an unextendible pair may now be rejected. The corollary preserves complete original solutions, not every base-legal partial state.

A rooted proof transfers only when translation is a proved automorphism of the same context and solution family, or when all relevant translations are separately covered. In the invariant case, translating a hypothetical solution back gives a contradiction at the rooted pair. Fixed boundaries or seeds can defeat that argument. Translating the context as well does not establish a claim in the original context.

No current corona label or finite-window failure is asserted to meet this premise. Budget exhaustion is unknown; boundary-dependent negatives and incomplete enumeration are not global proofs. A geometric reading also needs a faithful reduction to the point model. The corollary gives a possible route to certified failure markings, with no extra pair exclusions, and makes no claim about compactness or measured benefit.

Why a genuine triple exclusion is different

Fixed-state limitation. Suppose a triple \(S=\{c_1,c_2,c_3\}\) is base-capacity-legal and all its constituent pairs have consistent markings. At each point and channel, any two assigned values come from one coherent placement or from a compatible pair. They must agree. The entire union is therefore a partial function, so the triple is marking-consistent and hence legal. The argument works for any finite collection and does not depend on binary values or finite support.

A triple that markings cannot isolate

Take \(L=\mathbb Z\), types \(A,B,C\), and \(t_A=t_B=t_C=\tfrac13\mathbf1_{\{0\}}\). The coincident pair sums are \(\tfrac23\), while the coincident triple sum is \(1\). Require \(\{(A,x),(B,x),(C,x)\}\) to be forbidden while every constituent pair stays allowed.

The triple is base-legal. Any fixed equality marking allowing those pairs also allows the triple. Thus this higher-order rule has no exact representation in the stated marking model.

A triple already rejected by capacity

With \(t_A=t_B=t_C=\tfrac12\mathbf1_{\{0\}}\), each coincident pair sums to \(1\), but the triple sums to \(\tfrac32>1\).

Capacity itself rejects the triple, with no markings. The limitation therefore requires the triple to be base-capacity-legal; pairwise capacity legality alone does not suffice.

These are abstract mathematical examples, not executed fixtures. Pair allowance concerns the same fixed markings and placement identities. Separate existential choices of auxiliary states for each pair are outside the hypothesis. Added contextual or auxiliary states can change expressive power, but their projection to the original problem needs a separate soundness and completeness proof.
