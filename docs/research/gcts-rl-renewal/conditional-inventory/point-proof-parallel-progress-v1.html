A proved limitation of the current selection rule

The rule considers reachable proof roots in increasing source-row order, grows a fragment by adding its least external predecessor, and keeps the first two distinct eligible family keys per donor. Although fragments of up to eight internal nodes are allowed, this policy selects only two- and three-node fragments on a closed propositional proof containing just logical axioms and modus ponens, or selects none. Root's argument has received independent manual review; this is an analysis of the search policy, not a theorem found by the proof-search engine.

Read the argument line by line
  1. If no modus ponens row reaches the final conclusion, every reachable root is an axiom with no predecessors. Growth cannot reach the minimum size, so no family is selected.
  2. Otherwise let \(r\) be the earliest reachable modus ponens row, with predecessors \(a,b<r\). Both predecessors reach the conclusion. Either being modus ponens would contradict the choice of \(r\), so both are axioms.
  3. The parents are distinct: using the same row as antecedent and implication would require a finite formula \(F\) with \(F=(F\to C)\), impossible by syntax-tree size.
  4. Earlier axiom roots produce no eligible fragment. At \(r\), growth first adds \(\min(a,b)\), producing two internal rows and one boundary port. Adding the other parent produces three internal rows and no boundary ports. Both prefixes satisfy the declared structural limits.
  5. The full keys differ because their internal-node lists have different lengths. They fill the quota immediately. No larger or internally shared fragment is acquired. Overlap between the two saved fragments is distinct from sharing within one fragment.

The argument assumes finite propositional formula trees, backward references, and only the supported axiom and modus ponens rules in the final row's ancestor closure. A later encoder or resource refusal means a stopped library attempt; it does not authorize silently skipping to later fragments.
