What this experiment tests

Can a small reward-trained policy choose useful, context-dependent proof fragments from an automatically acquired inventory? Every fragment expands into ordinary primitive proof rows. A proposal can change their search order; it cannot remove the ordinary alternatives or decide that a proof is valid.

The diagram describes the fixed protocol. Completed stages and failures are listed below. Inputs are public authored compositional/operator-transfer examples, with intentionally shared primitive motifs; this is not a blind or family-disjoint benchmark.

GCTS authority

Exact point totals and marking agreement determine legality. Global dead points precede forced moves; earliest generation precedes branching. Cluster preferences retain all primitive fallback alternatives and exact rollback.

Validity authority

A completed candidate requires the Host kernel, the whole finite point region, and a fresh Native check of its flat primitive proof. Search cutoffs and Native budget exhaustion remain unknown.

What the policy actually learned
The policy has eight generic packet features and an abstention bias. It uses episode reward, a baseline computed before the update, and the sum of action score gradients. It receives no theorem or family identifier as a feature.
\[R=I_{\mathrm{Native\ accepted}}+0.05\,\max_s\frac{\#\{p:T_s(p)=1\}}{\#Q}-0.01\frac{A}{600}-0.01\frac{C}{20000}.\]
The maximum is over completed scans of actual live states, including a scan whose later audit is cut off before trace logging. It does not accumulate rolled-back placements. The work term is not clipped; total reward is not globally bounded. This is a heuristic learning signal, not a speedup certificate.
