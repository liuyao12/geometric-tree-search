Reusable lemmas are already central to these systems. Our possible contribution is a useful geometric constraint representation, adaptable cluster interfaces and a search policy that discovers effective compositions. Inventory reuse by itself is not evidence of an advantage over existing methods.

Measure what the markings and policy contribute.

First compare the reference point search, fixed ordering, frozen RL ordering, certified-marking variants and Z3 on the same complete finite point model. Retain a library-disabled control and a matched admitted-inventory control. This isolates the effects of representation, failure learning and ordering.

Separately compare public theorem statements with external provers under the same axiom theory and disclosed lemma availability. Give both sides equal wall-time and hardware budgets, and validate each final proof. A different proof calculus or formula bound must be reported; external semantic comparisons are distinct from matched point-model ablations.

Record solved and unknown counts, total time including learning and validation, attempted base placements, branches, backtracks, proof size and lemma use. Split by proof structure and source theorem, not only by renamed symbols. Freeze policies before evaluation. Report the cost of creating an inventory and the benefit of amortizing it across new problems separately.
