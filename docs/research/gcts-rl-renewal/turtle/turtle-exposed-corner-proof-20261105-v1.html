Why a finite exact Turtle substitution cannot close

Continue with whole-shell growth, compatible receptors and compound pairings.

Theorem for the lattice model. There is no closed substitution of finite Turtle clusters in which one common expansive similarity maps the lattice into itself and each inflated cluster is partitioned exactly into translated and allowed rotated or reflected copies from the library.

From the exposed corner to the obstruction

  1. Choose one exposed vertex. A generic linear functional reaches its maximum at one vertex \(v\) of the finite cluster. The nearby region lies in a wedge strictly narrower than \(180^\circ\). Its boundary rays belong to the six unoriented edge directions of the primitive Turtle. These directions divide the plane into twelve sectors of \(30^\circ\). The local region occupies a nonempty subset of at most five consecutive sectors. We include disconnected subsets.
  2. Check the finite list. The search covered all 192 possible subsets. The independent verifier rebuilds the legal alignments from absolute atom positions and uses signs of the six edge normals to check membership in the cone. At each node every legal placement covering the queried cell must occur as a child. Leaves have an empty domain. The checker never consults the search markings.
  3. Transfer a cone proof to a large cluster. The tree refers to finitely many samples, including every sample that could occur in an alignment of one of its queries. Translate these samples with \(kv\) for positive integer \(k\). Relative to an edge of the scaled cluster, the line value is affine in \(k\) in the translated coordinates. For lines through \(v\) the sign is already fixed. Every other line eventually has the sign of its nonzero slope. The finite samples then agree with the cone on which the proof was checked. Lattice translation preserves their residues and the list of possible alignments.
  4. Apply the proposed rule repeatedly. Any compatible similarity has angular part in multiples of \(30^\circ\), since an exposed straight edge of the inflated cluster must match a primitive edge direction. Such a similarity normalizes the allowed point actions, so the cluster rule can be iterated. An orientation-preserving similarity has a positive integral scalar power after twelve steps. An orientation-reversing similarity has one after two. Iteration would therefore give exact tilings at unbounded integral multipliers of every cluster. Step 3 excludes those tilings.

The argument applies to exact boundaries that are scaled copies of a fixed shape. The Turtle can tile the plane. The literature constructs combinatorial hierarchies, where large patches can have different geometric boundaries from the smaller shapes they represent. Those constructions and finite-region tilings with a movable boundary fall outside the theorem. Smith, Myers, Kaplan and Goodman-Strauss · Socolar

Every multiplier for the three-tile bank

The earlier finite screen covered 2,241 shapes at all eligible area ratios through \(48\). The two allowed similarity rays have area ratios \(k^2\) and \(3k^2\). Replaying saved proofs already covered every larger multiplier for 114 shapes on the first ray and 1,499 on the second.

The vertex search closed the remaining 2,127 and 742 cases. For each such shape, a cone proof was transferred to a concrete multiplier bound and checked on the full scaled polygon. The same proof also replayed at every integer multiplier between that bound and the finite screen. Thus all 2,241 three-tile shapes fail at every lattice-preserving similarity with area ratio greater than \(1\).
