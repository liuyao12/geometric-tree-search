Why two triples cannot share a piece

  1. Fix one piece in its lattice frame. Any threefold group containing it is determined by one of the two order-three rotations and the translation that places the next copy in edge contact. The third copy is forced. Exact supports check overlap and the last contact.
  2. Compare every rooted candidate with the necessary Turtle shell inventory. The shell checker requires all prescribed contacts to occur, excludes overlap, enforces adjacent shell compatibility, and checks that each required pair or single is outside all first-layer cores. A wider collar excludes the extra single shape and the possible three-core shape for rule 2.
  3. Two different triples sharing a piece appear together in the same rooted list. Nine candidate pairs survive the individual shell screen. Four are excluded by support overlap and four by incompatible immediate shells. The remaining pair is excluded after assigning four further shell layers around its prescribed pieces. Its finite failure tree has 49 nodes and replays against the full branch domains.

Conditional partition. In a whole-plane Turtle tiling, every finite collar required by the tiling exists. The exclusions therefore imply that the threefold groups can all be contracted at once. The surviving shapes describe the available triple types. A repeatable substitution still requires a rule for their next-scale neighborhoods and a construction of arbitrarily large compatible regions.
