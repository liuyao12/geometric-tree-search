What must fit at a receptor

A first shell contains a central Turtle and every tile that shares a positive-length edge segment with it. A receptor is one particular neighboring placement in the central tile's frame. Its domain consists of shell types that can be placed there while remaining consistent with the other surrounding shells.

  • 242 exact first shells
  • 50 all exposed segments individually supported
  • 37 a simultaneous second ring exists
  • 25 joint shell assignment survives iteration
  • 20 survive the deeper finite check

The earlier finite-patch campaign supplied 10 recurrent shell shapes from distinct training regions. A separate exact catalogue exhausts the full perimeter using 82 legal rooted edge contacts. Of the 50 shells with an individually supported boundary, 13 have no simultaneous second ring. Checking whole shells at adjacent receptors removes 12 more types. An independent replay of 18 deeper failed searches verifies five further exclusions. Each removal is necessary for any plane tiling in the lattice model because every tile would need a compatible surrounding shell.

The joint collar test verified 222 directed receptor choices on the 25-type inventory. A larger action adds all members of a neighboring shell in one step. Identical primitive placements are shared. Distinct placements must be disjoint. The positive checker rebuilds the contact graph from the complete rooted catalogue and verifies that every tile within the requested contact distance has its entire first shell.

Four rules for forming two-tile compounds

One shell has four opposite-hand neighbors. Each other shell has at most one. For all 222 admitted directed receptor choices, the determinant of the relative point action agrees with the product of the two shell types' handedness labels. Therefore the product of a tile's absolute handedness and its shell label is constant on every connected component of a compatible tiling.

Choose any one of the special shell's four roles. A special tile can pair with the neighbor at that role, and no ordinary tile can be chosen twice because its shell contains at most one opposite-hand neighbor. Does learning improve search?

Training updated a six-feature policy on 16 shell roots. The nine other roots supplied no updates. All methods used the same frontier rule, geometry and bounds. They differed only in the order in which they tried whole-shell actions. Shallow depths were three and four. The deeper test used six and eight.

Moving a boundary can disable a cut

Each of the 192 corner contradictions gives a finite required set \(Q\) and excluded set \(E\). A movable envelope is impossible if its mandatory region contains \(Q\) and its permitted region avoids \(E\). The checker uses those two sets and the exact branch list. It needs no description of the rest of the boundary.

The cuts rejected 24 of 24 new convex envelopes at margin zero and again at the first positive margin. They rejected eight at the second margin. The 128 tileable control patches triggered zero cuts. Margins are measured in the integer primitive-normal sample coordinates. A missed cut leaves the case open.
