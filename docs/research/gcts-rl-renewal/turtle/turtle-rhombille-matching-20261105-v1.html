Recover the marks from the polygon

James Smith's dual Turtle suggests looking for four congruent rhombs with two inward boundary edges. Seven vertices of the original polygon share one parity class. Completing their straight boundary at the only missing corner gives one four-rhomb footprint under these constraints. Testing its 28 edge colorings recovers one that reconstructs the exact Turtle polygon. Turtles, Hats and Spectres

Every occupied rhomb retains at least three of its four wedges, so two Turtles claiming the same rhomb would overlap. Every boundary wedge faces a unique neighboring rhomb. Opposite colors on an occupied seam put the wedge in the right tile. Around a hole the four neighbors contribute its four wedges.

All 114 neighbor roles in the necessary shell inventory preserve the grid phase. Across 25 exact patches, the bridge checks 7,978 Turtle placements, 31,912 occupied rhombs and 14,898 occupied seams. Every checked seam between distinct Turtles has opposite colors.

Decode three line words

Use the two adjacent cube layers with coordinate sums \(-2\) and \(-1\). A Turtle's hexagon writes the same bit on each of the three coordinate lines through its center. Its handedness gives the bit. A rhomb sees the two coordinates shared by its adjacent cubes. Equal bits give a hexagon rhomb. The order of unequal bits distinguishes a red rhomb from a hole, with the order reversed under reflection.

An exact model for generating new patches

Use the Fibonacci candidate's slope \(\alpha=(5-\sqrt{5})/10\) and letters \(b_i(n)=\lfloor(n+1)\alpha+\beta_i\rfloor-\lfloor n\alpha+\beta_i\rfloor\). The intercepts are nonintegral rationals with sum zero. A cube's integer band is \(k=\sum_i\{n_i\alpha+\beta_i\}-\alpha\sum_i n_i\), where braces denote fractional part.

Select every all-one cube center. Select an all-zero center in the lower layer only in band 2, or in the upper layer only in band 1. The checker enumerates all 288 local phase cases using exact arithmetic in \(\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})\). It verifies unique hexagon ownership, opposite colors across seams and two possible hexagon owners for each red rhomb. Selecting every uniform center gave ownership failures.

Where the plane proof stops

The red-rhomb graph has one vertex per selected hexagon and one edge per red rhomb. The tested finite windows produce trees that reach their boundary. Orient each tree away from a boundary root to choose the attachment at every other vertex. The guard excludes those unmatched roots from the coverage target.

Remaining global step. To obtain a plane tiling from this particular coloring, it would suffice to prove that every connected component of the infinite red-rhomb graph is an infinite tree or has exactly one cycle. Orient a tree away from a chosen end, or a unicyclic component away from its consistently oriented cycle. The four finite checks do not establish that graph property.

What happened to the third layer?

The earlier grouping approach yields two exact patterns that join a triple of single tiles to three four-tile cores. They transfer across both patch banks and give 27 checked contractions. A finite shell certificate excludes their only geometry-compatible shared-core case.
